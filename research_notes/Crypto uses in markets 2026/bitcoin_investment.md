# Bitcoin as an asset, and how investors and institutions hold crypto (state of things at 4 October 2026)

Conventions used in these notes. Each finding is tagged **(a)** well-established and safe to teach, **(b)** fast-changing figure that must carry an "as of" date, or **(c)** contested or commonly misstated, with the accurate version. Confidence is marked High / Medium / Low where it is not obviously High. "Checked 4 Oct 2026" means I queried the primary data source directly on that date (about 16:30 UTC). Percentages marked "computed" are my own arithmetic from the cited figures. No item here is investment advice or a forecast; forecasts found in sources were deliberately left out.

## 1. Supply and issuance: the 21 million cap, halvings, current subsidy, coins mined, lost coins

### Takeaway
Bitcoin's issuance is a fixed, code-enforced schedule: the per-block subsidy started at 50 BTC and halves every 210,000 blocks, which is what limits total supply to just under 21 million. As of 4 October 2026 the subsidy is 3.125 BTC per block, about 20.09 million BTC (95.7% of the cap) have been issued, and estimates of permanently lost coins range from roughly 1.5 million to nearly 4 million because "lost" cannot be observed directly, only inferred from coins that have not moved.

### Cited Findings

**(a) Well-established**
- The whitepaper describes Bitcoin as "a purely peer-to-peer version of electronic cash" allowing payments "directly from one party to another without going through a financial institution." — [Bitcoin whitepaper, HTML text](https://nakamotoinstitute.org/library/bitcoin/); [PDF](https://bitcoin.org/bitcoin.pdf)
- New coins enter circulation only through the first transaction in each block: "By convention, the first transaction in a block is a special transaction that starts a new coin owned by the creator of the block." Later, "once a predetermined number of coins have entered circulation, the incentive can transition entirely to transaction fees." — [Bitcoin whitepaper, section 6](https://nakamotoinstitute.org/library/bitcoin/)
- The subsidy halves every 210,000 blocks: 50 BTC (blocks 0 to 209,999), 25 BTC (210,000 to 419,999), 12.5 BTC (420,000 to 629,999), 6.25 BTC (630,000 to 839,999), 3.125 BTC (from 840,000). — [Bitcoin Wiki, Controlled supply](https://en.bitcoin.it/wiki/Controlled_supply)
- Exact halving times, read from the blocks themselves (checked 4 Oct 2026): block 210,000 on 28 Nov 2012 15:24 UTC — [block 210,000](https://mempool.space/block/000000000000048b95347e83192f69cf0366076336c639f9b7228e9ba171342e); block 420,000 on 9 Jul 2016 16:46 UTC — [block 420,000](https://mempool.space/block/000000000000000002cce816c0ab2c5c269cb081896b7dcb34b8422d6b74ffa1); block 630,000 on 11 May 2020 19:23 UTC — [block 630,000](https://mempool.space/block/000000000000000000024bead8df69990852c202db0e0097c1a12ea637d7e96d); block 840,000 on 20 Apr 2024 00:09 UTC — [block 840,000](https://mempool.space/block/0000000000000000000320283a032748cef8227873ff4872689bf23f1cda83a5)
- The first block (genesis block, height 0) is timestamped 3 Jan 2009 18:15 UTC. — [genesis block](https://mempool.space/block/000000000019d6689c085ae165831e934ff763ae46a2a6c172b3f1b60a8ce26f)
- The true maximum is slightly under 21 million: 20,999,999.9769 BTC, because amounts are counted in whole smallest units and rounded down at each halving. — [Bitcoin Wiki, Controlled supply](https://en.bitcoin.it/wiki/Controlled_supply)
- The next halving is at block 1,050,000, when the subsidy falls from 3.125 to 1.5625 BTC. — [IG UK, Bitcoin halving 2028](https://www.ig.com/uk/crypto-need-to-knows/bitcoin-halving-2028); [Nasdaq](https://www.nasdaq.com/articles/when-is-the-next-bitcoin-halving); consistent with the schedule in [Bitcoin Wiki](https://en.bitcoin.it/wiki/Controlled_supply) (pages read via search extracts, not opened individually)
- A few coins are provably unspendable: the 50 BTC in the genesis block "cannot be spent", and two early 50 BTC rewards were made unspendable by duplicate transaction IDs. — [Bitcoin Wiki, Controlled supply](https://en.bitcoin.it/wiki/Controlled_supply)

- Chain height 969,871 at 4 Oct 2026 16:28 UTC. — [mempool.space tip height](https://mempool.space/api/blocks/tip/height)
- Issued supply about 20,093,300 BTC on 4 Oct 2026, which is 95.68% of 21 million (computed from the height and the subsidy schedule; an independent supply counter returned 20,093,321 BTC, and CoinGecko showed 20,093,221 BTC circulating). — [blockchain.info total BTC](https://blockchain.info/q/totalbc); [CoinGecko bitcoin data](https://api.coingecko.com/api/v3/coins/bitcoin)
- The 20 millionth bitcoin was issued in block 939,999 on 9 Mar 2026 13:22 UTC (computed: 19,687,500 BTC issued by block 839,999, plus 100,000 blocks at 3.125 BTC; timestamp checked 4 Oct 2026). — [mempool.space block-height lookup](https://mempool.space/api/block-height/939999)
- Blocks remaining to the next halving: 80,129 on 4 Oct 2026. At ten minutes per block that is about 556 days, pointing to roughly April 2028; sources say "around March and April 2028". The date is not fixed because it depends on block height. — [mempool.space tip height](https://mempool.space/api/blocks/tip/height); [IG UK](https://www.ig.com/uk/crypto-need-to-knows/bitcoin-halving-2028); [BitRef halving countdown](https://bitref.com/halving/)
- Lost-coin estimates (all are models, none is a measurement):
  - Chainalysis, Nov 2017 (for Fortune): 2.78 to 3.79 million BTC lost. — [Fortune, 25 Nov 2017](https://fortune.com/2017/11/25/lost-bitcoins/); summarised in [BitGo](https://www.bitgo.com/resources/blog/bitcoins-invisible-burn-lost-coins-outpace-new-supply/)
  - Chainalysis, 2020: about 3.7 million BTC, defined as coins that had not moved from their address for five years or more. — [AMBCrypto summary](https://ambcrypto.com/why-bitcoin-watchers-need-to-pay-attention-to-lost-coins/); [BingX Learn summary](https://bingx.com/en/learn/article/how-much-bitcoin-is-lost-forever) (Medium: primary Chainalysis post could not be retrieved)
  - River, Sept 2023: 3.0 to 4.0 million; Ledger, Nov 2025: 2.3 to 3.7 million; BitGo's own synthesis: about 3.1 million central, 2.7 to 3.9 million central range, 2.3 to 5.25 million wider envelope. — [BitGo](https://www.bitgo.com/resources/blog/bitcoins-invisible-burn-lost-coins-outpace-new-supply/) (Medium: secondary compilation)
  - River's ownership page (updated 31 Dec 2025) lists about 1.6 million BTC as lost and counts Satoshi Nakamoto's estimated 968,452 BTC as a separate category. — [River, Who owns the most bitcoin](https://river.com/learn/who-owns-the-most-bitcoin/)
- Satoshi Nakamoto's holdings are themselves an estimate: about 1.1 million BTC from Sergio Lerner's 2013 "Patoshi pattern" analysis of early blocks; River uses 968,452 BTC. None of these coins has moved. — [Cointelegraph](https://cointelegraph.com/news/big-questions-does-satoshi-actually-own-1-million-bitcoin); [River](https://river.com/learn/who-owns-the-most-bitcoin/)

**(c) Contested or commonly misstated**
- Misstatement: "The whitepaper sets a 21 million limit and ten-minute blocks." Accurate: the whitepaper states neither. It speaks only of "a predetermined number of coins" and a difficulty "targeting an average number of blocks per hour". The 21 million cap comes from the subsidy schedule in the software (50 BTC halving every 210,000 blocks). — [Bitcoin whitepaper](https://nakamotoinstitute.org/library/bitcoin/); [Bitcoin Wiki](https://en.bitcoin.it/wiki/Controlled_supply)
- Misstatement: "There will be exactly 21 million bitcoin." Accurate: the ceiling is 20,999,999.9769 BTC, and the spendable total is lower still because some coins are provably unspendable and others are lost. — [Bitcoin Wiki](https://en.bitcoin.it/wiki/Controlled_supply)
- Misstatement: "The fourth halving was on 19 April 2024." Accurate: block 840,000 is timestamped 20 Apr 2024 00:09 UTC (01:09 BST); it was still 19 April in the Americas, which is why both dates circulate. Halving explainers returned by search (IG, Nasdaq and others) gave 19 April; I did not confirm which page uses which date. — [block 840,000](https://mempool.space/block/0000000000000000000320283a032748cef8227873ff4872689bf23f1cda83a5); [IG UK](https://www.ig.com/uk/crypto-need-to-knows/bitcoin-halving-2028); [Nasdaq](https://www.nasdaq.com/articles/when-is-the-next-bitcoin-halving)
- Misstatement: "X million bitcoin are lost" stated as fact. Accurate: published estimates span about 1.6 million to nearly 4 million. They differ mainly on (i) whether Satoshi's roughly 1 million coins count as lost, and (ii) what dormancy period is treated as lost. Dormant coins can be deliberate long-term holdings. — [BitGo](https://www.bitgo.com/resources/blog/bitcoins-invisible-burn-lost-coins-outpace-new-supply/); [River](https://river.com/learn/who-owns-the-most-bitcoin/)
- Misstatement: "The last bitcoin will be mined in 2140" as a precise date. Accurate: about 2140 is a projection (the wiki cites 7 May 2140 under a constant-hashrate assumption) and "remains speculative". — [Bitcoin Wiki](https://en.bitcoin.it/wiki/Controlled_supply)

**Mechanism for animators: issuance and the halving**
1. Each new block may begin with one special transaction that creates brand-new coins and pays them to whoever produced the block. — [whitepaper section 6](https://nakamotoinstitute.org/library/bitcoin/)
2. The amount created per block (the subsidy) was 50 BTC at launch on 3 Jan 2009. — [Bitcoin Wiki](https://en.bitcoin.it/wiki/Controlled_supply); [genesis block](https://mempool.space/block/000000000019d6689c085ae165831e934ff763ae46a2a6c172b3f1b60a8ce26f)
3. A counter of blocks (the block height) advances by one with each block. When it reaches a multiple of 210,000 the subsidy is cut in half: 50, 25, 12.5, 6.25, 3.125 (now), then 1.5625. The trigger is the block count, not a calendar date. — [Bitcoin Wiki](https://en.bitcoin.it/wiki/Controlled_supply)
4. Because blocks arrive about every ten minutes on average (2,016 blocks per two weeks is the target), 210,000 blocks take roughly four years. — [developer guide, block chain](https://developer.bitcoin.org/devguide/block_chain.html)
5. The running total therefore climbs in ever-smaller steps (10.5 million in the first era, then 5.25 million, 2.625 million and so on) and flattens just below 21 million around 2140. — [Bitcoin Wiki](https://en.bitcoin.it/wiki/Controlled_supply)

### Inferences
- At the target rate of 144 blocks a day, 3.125 BTC per block is about 450 BTC a day, roughly 164,000 BTC a year, or about 0.8% of the issued supply per year (computed from the schedule above).
- About 906,700 BTC (4.3% of the cap) remain to be issued as of 4 Oct 2026 (21,000,000 minus 20,093,350, computed).
- No person or company "performs" a halving. It follows from every node applying the same subsidy rule to every block; a block claiming more than the allowed amount would be rejected as invalid. This follows from the consensus design in the developer documentation but I did not fetch a sentence stating it verbatim.
- For a beginner lesson the safest phrasing is "just under 21 million" and "roughly every four years".

### Gaps
- I could not access Glassnode or Coin Metrics data directly, so there is no primary on-chain series here for dormant supply (for example coins unmoved for 5 or 10 years) as of 2026.
- The original 2020 Chainalysis post with the 3.7 million figure could not be retrieved (the URL now returns a blog index); only secondary summaries are cited.
- River's 2023 "3.0 to 4.0 million" and its 2025 "about 1.6 million" lost-coin figures appear to use different category definitions; I could not confirm River's stated methodology for either.

## 2. Mining: what proof-of-work miners do, difficulty adjustment, how miners are paid, energy use and mix

### Takeaway
Mining is a repeated guessing lottery, not problem-solving: machines hash an 80-byte block header over and over, changing a number each time, until one hash falls below a network-set target; the winner's block pays them the subsidy plus fees. The network retunes the target every 2,016 blocks to keep blocks about ten minutes apart, so more machines mean more electricity, not more bitcoin. Energy estimates disagree materially: Cambridge put annual use at 138 TWh (mid-2024) rising to about 190 TWh (December 2025), Digiconomist shows about 204 TWh, and their emissions estimates differ by more than a factor of two.

### Cited Findings

**(a) Well-established**
- Proof of work: "To prove you did some extra work to create a block, you must create a hash of the block header which does not exceed a certain value." — [developer guide, block chain](https://developer.bitcoin.org/devguide/block_chain.html)
- The header is 80 bytes with six fields: version (4 bytes), previous block header hash (32), merkle root hash (32), time (4), nBits, the encoded target (4), and nonce (4). It is hashed with SHA-256 applied twice. — [developer reference, block chain](https://developer.bitcoin.org/reference/block_chain.html)
- Difficulty adjustment: every 2,016 blocks the network compares the time those blocks took with the ideal of 1,209,600 seconds (two weeks) and raises or lowers difficulty proportionally, within a cap per adjustment. — [developer guide, block chain](https://developer.bitcoin.org/devguide/block_chain.html)
- Payment: the "block reward and transaction fees that come from mining that block" go to the miner or pool via the coinbase transaction; a coinbase output "cannot be spent ... for at least 100 blocks." — [developer guide, mining](https://developer.bitcoin.org/devguide/mining.html); [developer guide, block chain](https://developer.bitcoin.org/devguide/block_chain.html)
- Pools: the pool sets an easier target "a few orders of magnitude higher (less difficult) than the network difficulty"; miners submit "shares" proving work done and are paid "based on how many shares they generated." — [developer guide, mining](https://developer.bitcoin.org/devguide/mining.html)
- Competing blocks: "Multiple blocks can all have the same block height, as is common when two or more miners each produce a block at roughly the same time"; nodes "always follow the most difficult chain to recreate and throw away stale blocks belonging to shorter forks." — [developer guide, block chain](https://developer.bitcoin.org/devguide/block_chain.html)

**(b) Fast-changing (date every figure)**
- Difficulty 132.72 trillion after the retarget at block 969,696 on 3 Oct 2026; network hashrate about 955 EH/s (3-day estimate, 4 Oct 2026). — [mempool.space difficulty adjustments](https://mempool.space/api/v1/mining/difficulty-adjustments/3y); [mempool.space hashrate](https://mempool.space/api/v1/mining/hashrate/3d)
- The highest difficulty in the last three years was 155.97 trillion, set on 29 Oct 2025 at block 921,312; difficulty in October 2026 is about 15% below that (computed). — [mempool.space difficulty adjustments](https://mempool.space/api/v1/mining/difficulty-adjustments/3y)
- Cambridge Digital Mining Industry Report (published 28 Apr 2025; data around mid-2024): annual electricity use 138 TWh, about 0.5% of global consumption; emissions 39.8 MtCO2e; 52.4% "sustainable" energy (42.6% renewables plus 9.8% nuclear), up from 37.6% in 2022; natural gas 38.2% (largest single source, up from 25.0%); coal 8.9% (down from 36.6%). Based on a survey of 49 mining firms representing 48% of global mining activity; the US was 75.4% and Canada 7.1% of reported activity. — [Cambridge Judge Business School, 28 Apr 2025](https://jbs.cam.ac.uk/2025/cambridge-study-sustainable-energy-rising-in-bitcoin-mining/)
- Cambridge preview of the second edition (presented by CCAF researcher Alexander Neumueller at an energy conference; reported 25 Jul 2026): annualised electricity use about 190 TWh in December 2025, up 38% from 138 TWh in June 2024; emissions about 48 MtCO2e, up about 20%; low-carbon share 59.4% of the surveyed mix; hydropower now the largest single source, overtaking natural gas; survey covers a little over half of global hashrate; full report "expected later in 2026". — [The Energy Mag, 25 Jul 2026](https://www.theenergymag.com/news/market-news/bitcoin-energy-consumption-mix-cambridge-eif); [crypto.news](https://crypto.news/hydropower-overtakes-gas-as-bitcoin-mining-power-use-jumps-38/); [ForkLog](https://forklog.com/en/low-carbon-energy-share-in-bitcoin-mining-exceeds-59/) (Medium: trade-press reports of a presentation, not the published report)
- Digiconomist Bitcoin Energy Consumption Index (as displayed 4 Oct 2026): 204.44 TWh a year; 114.03 MtCO2; 22.14 kt e-waste; 3,222 GL fresh water. Method: assumes miners spend a share of revenue on electricity at 5 US cents per kWh. — [Digiconomist](https://digiconomist.net/bitcoin-energy-consumption)

**(c) Contested or commonly misstated**
- Misstatement: "Miners solve complex mathematical puzzles." Accurate: mining hardware "iterates through every possible value for the block header nonce and generates the corresponding hash"; it is brute-force trial and error, and checking a winning answer takes one hash. — [developer guide, mining](https://developer.bitcoin.org/devguide/mining.html)
- Misstatement: "More miners means bitcoin is produced faster." Accurate: the difficulty adjustment pulls block times back to the two-week-per-2,016-blocks target, so issuance stays on schedule. — [developer guide, block chain](https://developer.bitcoin.org/devguide/block_chain.html)
- Where estimates disagree: electricity (Cambridge about 190 TWh for Dec 2025 versus Digiconomist about 204 TWh in Oct 2026) is fairly close; emissions are not (Cambridge about 48 MtCO2e versus Digiconomist 114 MtCO2). — [The Energy Mag](https://www.theenergymag.com/news/market-news/bitcoin-energy-consumption-mix-cambridge-eif); [Digiconomist](https://digiconomist.net/bitcoin-energy-consumption)
- Misstatement: "Bitcoin mining is mostly renewable" or "mostly coal". Accurate: Cambridge's survey found 52.4% sustainable including nuclear (42.6% renewables alone) with gas the largest single source in mid-2024, and 59.4% low-carbon with hydropower largest in December 2025. These describe the surveyed firms, about half the network, skewed to North America and to firms willing to disclose, not the whole industry. — [Cambridge Judge Business School](https://jbs.cam.ac.uk/2025/cambridge-study-sustainable-energy-rising-in-bitcoin-mining/); [The Energy Mag](https://www.theenergymag.com/news/market-news/bitcoin-energy-consumption-mix-cambridge-eif); [ForkLog](https://forklog.com/en/low-carbon-energy-share-in-bitcoin-mining-exceeds-59/)
- Misstatement: "A cleaner mix means lower emissions." Accurate: per the Cambridge preview, the mix got cleaner while total emissions still rose about 20%, because consumption rose 38%. — [The Energy Mag](https://www.theenergymag.com/news/market-news/bitcoin-energy-consumption-mix-cambridge-eif)
- Misstatement seen in September 2026 coverage: "difficulty hit an all-time high of 132.76 trillion." Accurate: the record is 155.97 trillion from 29 Oct 2025. — claim at [pickaxe.io](https://pickaxe.io/resources/news/bitcoin-mining-difficulty-hits-all-time-high-on-sept-26); contradicted by [mempool.space difficulty history](https://mempool.space/api/v1/mining/difficulty-adjustments/3y)
- "Per-transaction" energy figures (Digiconomist shows 845 kWh per transaction) are published but contested as a framing; see Inferences. — [Digiconomist](https://digiconomist.net/bitcoin-energy-consumption)

**Mechanism for animators: one round of mining**
1. The miner's software gathers waiting transactions and builds a candidate block. It adds a coinbase transaction that pays the miner (or pool). — [developer guide, mining](https://developer.bitcoin.org/devguide/mining.html)
2. All the transactions are hashed together in pairs into a single 32-byte fingerprint, the merkle root. — [developer guide, mining](https://developer.bitcoin.org/devguide/mining.html)
3. The software assembles the 80-byte header: version, the previous block's hash (this is the "chain" link), the merkle root, the time, the target (nBits), and a nonce. — [developer reference](https://developer.bitcoin.org/reference/block_chain.html)
4. The machine runs the header through SHA-256 twice, producing a 256-bit number. If that number is at or below the target, the block is valid. If not, change the nonce and try again. — [developer reference](https://developer.bitcoin.org/reference/block_chain.html); [developer guide, block chain](https://developer.bitcoin.org/devguide/block_chain.html)
5. The nonce is only 4 bytes, so when every value has been tried the software changes an "extra nonce" inside the coinbase transaction, which produces "an updated block header with a new merkle root", and the guessing continues. — [developer guide, mining](https://developer.bitcoin.org/devguide/mining.html)
6. When a hash below the target is found, the completed block is "broadcast to the network for addition to the block chain". Other nodes verify it and build on it. — [developer guide, mining](https://developer.bitcoin.org/devguide/mining.html)
7. If two miners find blocks at nearly the same moment, two versions exist briefly; nodes follow whichever chain accumulates the most work and discard the stale block. — [developer guide, block chain](https://developer.bitcoin.org/devguide/block_chain.html)
8. The winner's coinbase transaction now pays them the subsidy (3.125 BTC) plus the fees from the included transactions, spendable after 100 further blocks. — [developer guide, block chain](https://developer.bitcoin.org/devguide/block_chain.html); [Bitcoin Wiki](https://en.bitcoin.it/wiki/Controlled_supply)
9. Every 2,016 blocks, the target is recalculated: blocks came faster than two weeks, the target gets harder; slower, easier. — [developer guide, block chain](https://developer.bitcoin.org/devguide/block_chain.html)

### Inferences
- Implied carbon intensity differs sharply between sources: Cambridge about 288 gCO2e/kWh for mid-2024 (39.8 Mt over 138 TWh) and about 253 g/kWh for Dec 2025 (48 Mt over 190 TWh); Digiconomist about 558 g/kWh (114.03 Mt over 204.44 TWh). The emissions gap is therefore mostly an energy-mix assumption gap, not an electricity gap (computed).
- Energy use is driven by how much hashing miners find profitable (subsidy, fees, price, electricity cost), not by the number of transactions; a block uses the same work whether full or nearly empty. A per-transaction figure is a division, not a marginal cost. This follows from the mechanism above; I believe the CBECI FAQ makes the same point but did not retrieve it, so verify there before teaching it as Cambridge's view.
- The fall in difficulty from the October 2025 record is consistent with some miners switching off after the price fall described in section 5; I found no source establishing the cause, so treat as unverified.
- An animator should show the target as a line that moves (down when hashing power rises), with guesses landing above it until one lands below.

### Gaps
- The live CBECI index figure (central, lower and upper bound) could not be read: the page at [ccaf.io/cbnsi/cbeci](https://ccaf.io/cbnsi/cbeci) loads its data dynamically. A secondary site quoted a CBECI range of about 80 to 170 TWh around a 138 TWh best guess, but I judged that source unreliable and have not used it.
- The full second-edition Cambridge Digital Mining Industry Report did not appear to be published as of 4 Oct 2026; the December 2025 figures above are preview figures and could change.
- The developer guide's wording on the adjustment cap was returned as "up to 300%" increase and "up to 75%" decrease; I did not verify the exact limit (commonly described as a factor of four either way) against the source code.
- No sourced figure here for the current split of miner revenue between subsidy and fees.

## 3. Holders and vehicles: spot Bitcoin and Ether ETPs, company treasuries, government holdings, share of supply

### Takeaway
US regulators approved spot bitcoin exchange-traded products on 10 January 2024 and spot ether products later that year; by late 2025 tracked institutions (funds, companies, governments and others) held just over 4 million BTC, about 19% of the 21 million cap, with funds around 7% and public companies around 5%. Figures for 2026 are patchier: one company, Strategy, held 847,363 BTC (about 4% of the cap) in mid-June 2026, and US spot bitcoin ETFs were reported at about $101 billion in early September 2026, but government holdings in particular differ by a factor of two between trackers.

### Cited Findings

**(a) Well-established**
- On 10 January 2024 the SEC "approved the listing and trading of a number of spot bitcoin exchange-traded product (ETP) shares", following a court ruling that the SEC had "failed to adequately explain its reasoning" in rejecting Grayscale's application. — [SEC Chair statement, 10 Jan 2024](https://www.sec.gov/newsroom/speeches-statements/gensler-statement-spot-bitcoin-011023)
- The same statement said: "we did not approve or endorse bitcoin" and called bitcoin "primarily a speculative, volatile asset that's also used for illicit activity". — [SEC Chair statement, 10 Jan 2024](https://www.sec.gov/newsroom/speeches-statements/gensler-statement-spot-bitcoin-011023)
- Spot ether products: the SEC approved the exchange rule changes (Form 19b-4) on 23 May 2024, cleared the issuers' registration statements on 22 July 2024, and trading began on 23 July 2024 on Cboe, Nasdaq and NYSE Arca. — [Baker McKenzie, 23 Jul 2024](https://blockchain.bakermckenzie.com/2024/07/23/ethereum-etfs-to-start-trading-today-in-the-united-states/); [Vedder Price](https://vedderprice.com/sec-approves-exchange-listings-for-spot-ether-etps); [Troutman Pepper](https://www.troutman.com/insights/sec-approves-spot-ether-etfs.html)

## 4. UK access: what a UK retail investor can legally buy as of October 2026

### Takeaway
UK adults can legally buy cryptoassets directly, and since 8 October 2025 can also buy crypto exchange-traded notes listed on an FCA-recognised UK exchange; crypto derivatives remain banned for retail. Crypto ETNs can be held in pensions, and in ISAs only via the Innovative Finance ISA since 6 April 2026 (earlier stocks and shares ISA holdings were reclassified, not forced out). Neither route carries FSCS compensation, and the full FCA authorisation regime for crypto firms does not start until 25 October 2027.

### Cited Findings

**(a) Well-established**
- The FCA banned the sale, marketing and distribution to retail consumers of derivatives (CFDs, options, futures) and exchange-traded notes referencing unregulated transferable cryptoassets, announced 6 Oct 2020, effective 6 Jan 2021, citing no reliable basis for valuation, market abuse and financial crime, extreme volatility and inadequate consumer understanding. — [FCA press release, 6 Oct 2020](https://www.fca.org.uk/news/press-releases/fca-bans-sale-crypto-derivatives-retail-consumers)
- The FCA announced on 1 Aug 2025 that retail consumers could access crypto ETNs from 8 Oct 2025, provided they are "traded on an FCA-approved, UK-based investment exchange (a Recognised Investment Exchange or RIE)". Financial promotion rules and the Consumer Duty apply; there is no Financial Services Compensation Scheme cover. — [FCA press release, 1 Aug 2025](https://www.fca.org.uk/news/press-releases/fca-opens-retail-access-crypto-etns)
- "The FCA's ban on retail access to cryptoasset derivatives will remain in place." — [FCA press release, 1 Aug 2025](https://www.fca.org.uk/news/press-releases/fca-opens-retail-access-crypto-etns)
- ISA and pension rules (HMRC policy paper, 8 Oct 2025): "Initially, cETNs will be automatically eligible for inclusion in stocks and shares ISAs." "From 6 April 2026, they will be reclassified as qualifying investments within the Innovative Finance ISA (IFISA)." "Any cETNs that have been included in stocks and shares ISAs before 6 April 2026 will be treated as qualifying IFISA investments from that date." "The government is allowing cETNs to be held within registered pension schemes from 8 October 2025." The government will keep this "under review with a view to including them in the stocks and shares ISA at a later date as the market matures." — [HMRC policy paper](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/tax-treatment-of-cryptoasset-exchange-traded-notes/tax-treatment-of-cryptoasset-exchange-traded-notes-policy)
- HMRC describes cETNs as "traditional financial instruments on which the return is tied to the performance of certain cryptoassets". — [KPMG summary of HMRC policy](https://kpmg.com/uk/en/insights/tax/tmd-tax-treatment-of-cryptoasset-exchange-traded-notes.html)
- London Stock Exchange admission criteria for crypto ETNs: physically backed, non-leveraged, underlying must be Bitcoin and/or Ether with a reliable public price, and the underlying held wholly or principally in cold storage or equivalent. — [LSE crypto ETN admission factsheet 2025](https://docs.londonstockexchange.com/sites/default/files/documents/crypto-etn-admission-factsheet-2025.pdf) (Medium: read via search extract, PDF not opened)
- Buying crypto directly: the FCA's consumer page says "crypto is largely unregulated in the UK", "you should be prepared to lose all the money you have invested" and "you should not expect any kind of compensation to cover any form of crypto-related losses". Promotions must carry prominent risk warnings and must not offer free gifts or incentives. — [FCA InvestSmart, crypto basics (updated 29 Jan 2026)](https://www.fca.org.uk/investsmart/crypto-basics)
- Tax outside wrappers: Capital Gains Tax may be due when you dispose of cryptoassets by selling them, "exchanging them for a different type of cryptoasset", "using them to pay for goods or services" or giving them away (with exceptions for spouses, civil partners and charity). — [HMRC guidance (updated 29 May 2025)](https://www.gov.uk/guidance/check-if-you-need-to-pay-tax-when-you-sell-cryptoassets)

**(b) Fast-changing (date every figure)**
- New regime timeline: the Financial Services and Markets Act 2000 (Cryptoassets) Regulations 2026 were made on 4 Feb 2026; "cryptoasset firms can now apply for authorisation"; "the new cryptoasset regime is expected to come into force on 25 October 2027." Until then firms operate under Money Laundering Regulations registration only. — [FCA, new regime for cryptoasset regulation](https://www.fca.org.uk/firms/new-regime-cryptoasset-regulation) (checked 4 Oct 2026)
- The application window opened 30 Sept 2026 and closes 28 Feb 2027; existing money-laundering registrations do not convert automatically. — [EisnerAmper, Sept 2026](https://www.eisneramper.com/insights/cryptocurrency-insights/fca-cryptoasset-authorization-0926/) (Medium: professional-services summary read via search extract; the FCA page confirms only that applications are open)
- The FCA published final rules in June 2026 (PS26/9 admissions, disclosures and market abuse; PS26/10 stablecoin issuance; PS26/11 regulated cryptoasset activities; PS26/12 prudential; PS26/13 Handbook application), taking effect October 2027; "FSCS protection will not be extended to cover cryptoassets." — [KPMG, Jul 2026](https://kpmg.com/xx/en/our-insights/regulatory-insights/the-uk-finalised-regulatory-regime-for-cryptoassets.html)
- UK ownership: 8% of UK adults held crypto in 2025, down from 12% in 2024; awareness 91%; 73% of users bought through a centralised exchange; 9% paid with a credit card or existing credit facility (down from 14%). Fieldwork 5 Aug to 2 Sept 2025, published 16 Dec 2025. — [FCA research note](https://www.fca.org.uk/publications/research-notes/cryptoassets-consumer-research-2025); [full report PDF](https://www.fca.org.uk/publication/research-notes/cryptoasset-consumer-research-2025-wave-6.pdf); [The Block](https://www.theblock.co/post/382722/uk-crypto-ownership-falls-2025-fca)
- Practical ISA availability after April 2026: industry body CryptoUK said no UK platform offered both crypto ETNs and an Innovative Finance ISA in one place; one platform (Stratiphy) was later reported as authorised to do so. — [CryptoUK](https://cryptouk.io/resources/the-uk-opened-the-door-to-crypto-etns-then-closed-it-through-the-tax-system/); [The Block](https://www.theblock.co/amp/post/398415/uk-investors-regain-tax-free-access-to-crypto-etns-via-innovative-finance-isa-route) (Medium-Low: trade and industry sources; current platform availability not verified)
- First retail listings: issuers including 21Shares, Bitwise and WisdomTree opened UK retail access to bitcoin and ether products in October 2025. — [The Block via TradingView](https://ru.tradingview.com/news/the_block:54dfacbad094b:0-21shares-bitwise-and-wisdomtree-open-uk-retail-access-to-bitcoin-and-ethereum-etps-following-fca-approval) (Medium-Low: exact first trading dates not verified)

**(c) Contested or commonly misstated**
- Misstatement: "You can hold bitcoin in an ISA." Accurate: coins themselves cannot go in an ISA. Crypto ETNs could be bought in a stocks and shares ISA only between 8 Oct 2025 and 5 Apr 2026; since 6 Apr 2026 they qualify only for the Innovative Finance ISA, with pre-existing holdings reclassified. — [HMRC policy paper](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/tax-treatment-of-cryptoasset-exchange-traded-notes/tax-treatment-of-cryptoasset-exchange-traded-notes-policy)
- Misstatement: "Crypto ETNs on the London Stock Exchange are protected by the FSCS." Accurate: the FCA states there is no FSCS cover. — [FCA press release, 1 Aug 2025](https://www.fca.org.uk/news/press-releases/fca-opens-retail-access-crypto-etns)
- Misstatement: "An FCA-registered crypto firm is FCA-regulated like a bank or broker." Accurate: until 25 Oct 2027 registration is under the Money Laundering Regulations only, and the FCA says crypto "remains high risk with no protections if something goes wrong." — [FCA new regime page](https://www.fca.org.uk/firms/new-regime-cryptoasset-regulation); [FCA InvestSmart](https://www.fca.org.uk/investsmart/crypto-basics)
- Misstatement: "UK investors can now buy Bitcoin ETFs." Accurate: the UK retail product is an exchange-traded note on a UK recognised exchange. US-listed spot bitcoin ETFs have not been available to UK retail clients because they lack the required UK Key Information Document. — [FCA press release](https://www.fca.org.uk/news/press-releases/fca-opens-retail-access-crypto-etns); [The Block Learn](https://www.theblock.co/learn/285413/can-uk-investors-buy-us-spot-bitcoin-etfs); [Interactive Brokers knowledge base](https://ibkb.interactivebrokers.com/node/2993) (Medium: the US-ETF sources date from 2024; I did not confirm nothing changed in 2026)
- Misstatement: "The FCA lifting the ban means the FCA thinks crypto is safe." Accurate: the FCA said products "have become more mainstream and better understood" and that "consumers should ensure they understand the risks before deciding to invest." — [FCA press release, 1 Aug 2025](https://www.fca.org.uk/news/press-releases/fca-opens-retail-access-crypto-etns)

**Mechanism for animators: two UK routes to exposure**
1. Direct route: a person opens an account with a crypto firm, pays pounds, and the firm credits coins, which can be left with the firm or withdrawn to the person's own wallet. Most UK users (73%) buy via a centralised exchange. No FSCS cover. — [FCA research note](https://www.fca.org.uk/publications/research-notes/cryptoassets-consumer-research-2025); [FCA InvestSmart](https://www.fca.org.uk/investsmart/crypto-basics)
2. ETN route: an issuer holds bitcoin or ether in cold storage and issues notes listed on a UK recognised exchange; the investor buys the note through an investment platform, and the note's return is tied to the cryptoasset's price. The investor holds a listed security, not coins. No FSCS cover. — [LSE factsheet](https://docs.londonstockexchange.com/sites/default/files/documents/crypto-etn-admission-factsheet-2025.pdf); [KPMG summary of HMRC policy](https://kpmg.com/uk/en/insights/tax/tmd-tax-treatment-of-cryptoasset-exchange-traded-notes.html); [FCA press release](https://www.fca.org.uk/news/press-releases/fca-opens-retail-access-crypto-etns)
3. Wrappers: the ETN, not the coin, can sit inside a registered pension, or an Innovative Finance ISA. — [HMRC policy paper](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/tax-treatment-of-cryptoasset-exchange-traded-notes/tax-treatment-of-cryptoasset-exchange-traded-notes-policy)

### Inferences
- "What remains banned for retail" as of October 2026 is, on the evidence found: crypto derivatives (CFDs, futures, options), plus crypto ETNs that are not on a UK recognised exchange. On the London Stock Exchange the admission criteria also exclude leveraged products and underlyings other than bitcoin and ether.
- Because the HMRC paper treats pre-April 2026 stocks and shares ISA holdings as IFISA investments, some investors hold crypto ETNs that still sit physically on a stocks and shares ISA platform. Lessons should not imply new purchases are possible that way.
- The KPMG note says non-UK clients will not have Financial Ombudsman access under the new regime, which implies UK clients of authorised firms will from October 2027; I did not verify this against the FCA policy statements.

### Gaps
- Whether the FCA's proposed restriction on buying crypto with credit cards or other borrowing was confirmed in the June 2026 final rules: sources found describe it as a proposal and as a restriction in different places; not confirmed. Do not teach as settled.
- I did not verify from a fetched primary source that crypto ETNs are legally debt securities carrying issuer credit risk (the standard description); confirm against an issuer prospectus or the FCA Handbook before teaching.
- The Morningstar UK article on ISA rule changes returned an access error, and I found no HMRC update after 8 Oct 2025; a later change to ISA eligibility in 2026 cannot be ruled out.
- Which mainstream UK platforms offer crypto ETNs, and in which account types, as of October 2026 was not established.
- Whether the UK's replacement for the Key Information Document rules has changed access to US-listed ETFs in 2026 was not established.

## 5. Volatility: major drawdowns, and the evidence on "digital gold" and inflation-hedge claims

### Takeaway
Bitcoin has repeatedly fallen 75% to 93% from a peak (2011, 2013 to 2015, 2017 to 2018, 2021 to 2022), and from its October 2025 record of about $126,000 it fell about 54% to roughly $58,000 by 1 July 2026 before recovering to about $85,000 (32% below the record) on 4 October 2026. Peer-reviewed evidence does not support calling it a safe haven: it fell in step with shares in the March 2020 crash and its correlation with shares rose sharply from 2020; evidence on inflation hedging is mixed, with at least one study for and others against.

### Cited Findings

**(a) Well-established**
- Earlier peak-to-trough falls: 2011, about $30 to about $2, 93%, roughly 160 days; 2013 to 2015, about 85% over roughly 410 days from the late-November 2013 peak; 2017 to 2018, about $19,000 to about $3,250, 83% to 84%, over about a year; 2021 to 2022, 75% to 77% to a low of $15,460 on 21 Nov 2022 during the FTX collapse. — [CryptoSlate](https://cryptoslate.com/analysis-of-previous-bitcoin-bottoms-suggests-more-pain-to-come/); [NYDIG](https://nydig.com/research/charting-drawdowns-during-up-cycles); [Bitcoin Education](https://bitcoineducation.substack.com/p/when-will-the-bitcoin-price-bottom) (Medium: exact prices and percentages vary by exchange and data provider)
- March 2020: "Bitcoin does not act as a safe haven, instead decreasing in price in lockstep with the S&P 500 as the crisis develops. When held alongside the S&P 500, even a small allocation to Bitcoin substantially increases portfolio downside risk." — [Conlon and McGee, Finance Research Letters 35 (2020)](https://ideas.repec.org/a/eee/finlet/v35y2020ics1544612320304244.html)
- Mixed finding: "Bitcoin appreciates against inflation (or inflation expectation) shocks, confirming its inflation-hedging property claimed by investors. However, unlike gold, Bitcoin prices decline in response to financial uncertainty shocks, rejecting the safe-haven quality." — [Choi and Shin, Finance Research Letters 46 (2022)](https://ideas.repec.org/a/eee/finlet/v46y2022ipbs1544612321003810.html)
- IMF staff found the correlation of daily moves between bitcoin and the S&P 500 was 0.01 in 2017 to 2019 and 0.36 in 2020 to 2021, higher than the correlation between shares and gold, investment-grade bonds or major currencies. — [IMF blog, 11 Jan 2022](https://www.imf.org/en/Blogs/Articles/2022/01/11/crypto-prices-move-more-in-sync-with-stocks-posing-new-risks); figures as reported by [Chief Investment Officer magazine](https://www.ai-cio.com/news/rising-crypto-equity-correlation-raises-bitcoin-stability-concerns/) (Medium: IMF page blocked my fetch; figures taken from secondary coverage)
- The US securities regulator described bitcoin as "primarily a speculative, volatile asset". — [SEC Chair statement, 10 Jan 2024](https://www.sec.gov/newsroom/speeches-statements/gensler-statement-spot-bitcoin-011023)

**(b) Fast-changing (date every figure)**
- All-time high: $126,080 on 6 Oct 2025 per CoinGecko; $126,223 per 21Shares. — [CoinGecko bitcoin data](https://api.coingecko.com/api/v3/coins/bitcoin); [21Shares, 8 Jul 2026](https://www.21shares.com/de/insights/bitcoin-cycle-bottom-2026-entry-point)
- 2026 low so far: about $57,950 on 1 Jul 2026, reported as the lowest in 652 days; a tracker's 30 Jun 2026 reference price was $58,398.25; a daily snapshot for 1 Jul 2026 shows $58,551. From $126,080 to $57,950 is a 54% fall (computed). — [Finbold](https://finbold.com/bitcoin-price-today-july-2026/); [cryptonews.net](https://cryptonews.net/news/bitcoin/33081919/); [BitcoinTreasuries.net June 2026 report](https://assets-prod.bitcointreasuries.net/files/019f3e6a-8302-7000-b3f9-619db5e9668e.pdf); [mempool.space historical price](https://mempool.space/api/v1/historical-price?currency=USD&timestamp=1782864000) (Medium on the exact $57,950 intraday low, which comes from news search extracts; the two reference prices near $58,400 to $58,600 were read directly)

- Gold and bitcoin diverged in 2025 to 2026: CoinShares research (via ETF Trends) reported bitcoin falling as the Federal Reserve shrank its balance sheet through 2025 while gold rallied; a blog reported gold above $5,600 in early 2026 while bitcoin fell below $74,000. — [ETF Trends, CoinShares content](https://www.etftrends.com/coinshares-content-hub/bitcoin-lagged-gold-fed-tightened-2025/); [BlockEden, 8 Feb 2026](https://blockeden.xyz/blog/2026/02/08/gold-vs-bitcoin-safe-haven-divergence/) (Low-Medium: industry sources, gold price not verified)

**(c) Contested or commonly misstated**
- Claim: "Bitcoin is digital gold." Status: a label, used even in the US executive order, not an established finding. Evidence against the safe-haven part: Conlon and McGee (2020); Choi and Shin (2022) on uncertainty shocks; IMF (2022) on rising equity correlation; the 2025 to 2026 divergence from gold. — [White House order](https://www.whitehouse.gov/presidential-actions/2025/03/establishment-of-the-strategic-bitcoin-reserve-and-united-states-digital-asset-stockpile/); [Conlon and McGee](https://ideas.repec.org/a/eee/finlet/v35y2020ics1544612320304244.html); [Choi and Shin](https://ideas.repec.org/a/eee/finlet/v46y2022ipbs1544612321003810.html); [IMF blog](https://www.imf.org/en/Blogs/Articles/2022/01/11/crypto-prices-move-more-in-sync-with-stocks-posing-new-risks)
- Claim: "Bitcoin is an inflation hedge." Status: contested. For: Choi and Shin (2022) find it appreciates after inflation shocks. Against: Pinchuk's 2023 study "Bitcoin Does Not Hedge Inflation" finds prices tend to fall in response to inflation surprises. - Misstatement: "Bitcoin always falls about 80% in a bear market." Accurate: four past falls ranged from 75% to 93%; the 2025 to 2026 fall reached about 54% at its low to date. Past falls do not determine future ones, and nobody can know whether the July 2026 low will stand. — [CryptoSlate](https://cryptoslate.com/analysis-of-previous-bitcoin-bottoms-suggests-more-pain-to-come/); [21Shares](https://www.21shares.com/de/insights/bitcoin-cycle-bottom-2026-entry-point)

- UK financial stability view: the Bank of England's Financial Policy Committee has said direct risks to UK financial stability from cryptoassets were limited but would grow with scale and interconnection (position set out in 2022); its July 2026 Financial Stability Report was published with the record of its 26 June 2026 meeting. — [Slaughter and May summary](https://thelens.slaughterandmay.com/post/102hls8/cryptoassets-and-financial-stability-boe-outlines-key-risks-and-approach); [A&O Shearman, July 2026](https://finreg.aoshearman.com/boes-financial-policy-committee-publishes-july-2026-financial-stability-report) (Medium-Low for 2026: I did not read the 2026 record's crypto wording)

### Inferences
- Using a peak of about $69,000 (November 2021) and the $15,460 low gives a 77.6% fall; sources quoting 75% appear to use closing or weekly prices. A lesson should say "about three-quarters" or cite one named data source.
- The 2021 to 2022 fall happened during the highest inflation in decades in the UK and US, which is the simplest counter-example to "inflation hedge". The inflation figures themselves are not sourced here; take them from the Office for National Statistics before using this point.
- The honest beginner-level summary is: bitcoin has been far more volatile than shares or gold, has tended to fall alongside shares in market stress since 2020, and research on inflation hedging disagrees.
- The correlation evidence cited is mostly from 2020 to 2022; it shows the claim was not supported then, not what correlations are in 2026.

### Gaps
- No peer-reviewed or central-bank study covering 2024 to 2026 correlations was retrieved; the 2025 to 2026 gold divergence rests on industry commentary.
- No sourced annualised volatility figure (for bitcoin versus shares or gold) was found in this session.
- The Bank of England's 2026 wording on unbacked cryptoassets was not retrieved from bankofengland.co.uk.

## 6. Ether and other large assets: only what is needed to show how they differ from Bitcoin as assets

### Takeaway
Ether differs from bitcoin on the three points a beginner lesson needs: it has no fixed supply cap, its network has been secured by staking rather than mining since 15 September 2022, and it is used to pay fees for running applications rather than serving mainly as money. Its supply can rise or fall because new ether is issued to validators while part of every fee is destroyed.

### Cited Findings

**(a) Well-established**
- Ether "is the native cryptocurrency that powers the Ethereum network. It's used to pay transaction fees (known as 'gas') for using the network and the applications built on top of it, to secure the network through staking." — [ethereum.org, ETH](https://ethereum.org/en/eth/)
- "Unlike Bitcoin's fixed 21 million cap, ETH has dynamic supply mechanics": new ETH "is issued to reward network validators at a limited rate calculated by the protocol", while "a portion of every transaction fee is permanently 'burned'". — [ethereum.org, ETH](https://ethereum.org/en/eth/)
- "The Merge was executed on September 15, 2022", joining Ethereum's original chain with its proof-of-stake consensus layer; "Ethereum's energy consumption dropped by an estimated 99.95%". — [ethereum.org, The Merge](https://ethereum.org/en/roadmap/merge/)
- Before the Merge miners were issued about 13,000 ETH a day; afterwards only validator issuance remained, "dropping total new ETH issuance by ~88%". Fee burning "went live with the London upgrade in August 2021". — [ethereum.org, issuance](https://ethereum.org/en/roadmap/merge/issuance/)
- Ether has no maximum supply recorded by data providers, whereas bitcoin's is listed as 21,000,000. — [CoinGecko ethereum data](https://api.coingecko.com/api/v3/coins/ethereum); [CoinGecko bitcoin data](https://api.coingecko.com/api/v3/coins/bitcoin)
- Spot ether exchange-traded products have traded in the US since 23 July 2024. — [Baker McKenzie](https://blockchain.bakermckenzie.com/2024/07/23/ethereum-etfs-to-start-trading-today-in-the-united-states/)
- In the UK, retail crypto ETNs admitted to the London Stock Exchange may reference only bitcoin and/or ether. — [LSE factsheet](https://docs.londonstockexchange.com/sites/default/files/documents/crypto-etn-admission-factsheet-2025.pdf)

**(b) Fast-changing (date every figure)**
- Ether in circulation: 122.10 million ETH; price $2,698 (£2,042); market value about $329 billion, against bitcoin's about $1.71 trillion, all on 4 Oct 2026. Ether's all-time high was $4,946 on 24 Aug 2025. — [CoinGecko ethereum data](https://api.coingecko.com/api/v3/coins/ethereum); [CoinGecko price API](https://api.coingecko.com/api/v3/simple/price?ids=bitcoin,ethereum&vs_currencies=usd,gbp&include_market_cap=true)
- Largest listed-company ether holder: BitMine with 5.70 million ETH as of 29 Jun 2026. — [BitcoinTreasuries.net June 2026 report](https://assets-prod.bitcointreasuries.net/files/019f3e6a-8302-7000-b3f9-619db5e9668e.pdf)
- ethereum.org's issuance example ("approximately 1,700 ETH/day, based on about 14 million total ETH staked") is a dated illustration, not a current figure. — [ethereum.org, issuance](https://ethereum.org/en/roadmap/merge/issuance/)
- Stablecoins are being regulated in the UK as a separate category: the Bank of England published a policy statement and draft Code of Practice on sterling systemic stablecoins on 22 Jun 2026, and the FCA's final stablecoin issuance rules are PS26/10. — [Regulation Tomorrow, June 2026](https://www.regulationtomorrow.com/2026/06/boe-publishes-a-policy-statement-and-consultation-on-a-draft-code-of-practice-in-relation-to-sterling-denominated-systemic-stablecoins/); [KPMG, Jul 2026](https://kpmg.com/xx/en/our-insights/regulatory-insights/the-uk-finalised-regulatory-regime-for-cryptoassets.html)

**(c) Contested or commonly misstated**
- Misstatement: "Ether is deflationary" (or "ultrasound money") as a fixed property. Accurate: supply falls only when fees burned exceed new issuance; ethereum.org's own example says net inflation reaches "zero or less" only on days when average gas prices are high enough (it cites 16 gwei for its example). — [ethereum.org, issuance](https://ethereum.org/en/roadmap/merge/issuance/)
- Misstatement: "All cryptocurrencies are mined." Accurate: Ethereum has not used mining since 15 Sept 2022. — [ethereum.org, The Merge](https://ethereum.org/en/roadmap/merge/)
- Misstatement: "Ethereum is green" as an unqualified claim. Accurate: ethereum.org reports an estimated 99.95% fall in energy use at the Merge; this is the project's own estimate. — [ethereum.org, The Merge](https://ethereum.org/en/roadmap/merge/)

### Inferences
- For a side-by-side graphic: bitcoin, cap just under 21 million, proof-of-work mining, issuance halves about every four years; ether, no cap, proof-of-stake validators, issuance set by protocol and partly offset by fee burning.
- Bitcoin's market value was about five times ether's on 4 Oct 2026 (computed from the CoinGecko figures above).
- The UK exchange rule limiting retail ETNs to bitcoin and ether is itself a useful, sourced way to explain which assets regulators have been willing to admit for retail products.

- Whether US spot ether ETPs are permitted to stake their holdings as of 2026 was not researched.
- No sourced facts were gathered on other large assets (for example stablecoins such as USDT and USDC, or Solana and XRP) beyond the UK regulatory references above; these need their own sourcing if lessons cover them.
- Reliable 2026 figures for ether held by ETPs were not obtained (see the inconsistency noted in section 3).
