## 1. Lending and borrowing: how a protocol such as Aave works, how big it is, who borrows, and what has gone wrong

### Takeaway
On-chain lending is pooled, over-collateralised, and enforced by code: suppliers deposit into a pool, borrowers lock collateral worth more than they borrow, interest rates move automatically with pool utilisation, and anyone can liquidate a position whose "health factor" falls below 1. Aave is the largest protocol (about $19.3bn net value locked and $13.2bn borrowed on 4 Oct 2026); its own contracts have processed billions in liquidations without failing, but it has still been hit by losses that originated elsewhere (the April 2026 Kelp rsETH bridge exploit), and the 2022 collapses of Celsius, Voyager, BlockFi and Genesis were failures of centralised companies, not of DeFi lending code.

### Cited Findings

#### Step-by-step mechanism (for the animator)
1. **Supply.** A user sends an asset (e.g. USDC or ETH) to the protocol's pool smart contract. In return, "aTokens (e.g., aUSDC, aWETH) are minted as interest-bearing ERC-20 tokens whose balance increases over time from borrowing activity in the pool." [P] — [Aave V3 Overview](https://aave.com/docs/aave-v3/overview)
2. **Where supplier interest comes from.** "Supplier yields are funded by borrower interest net of the reserve factor" (the reserve factor is the slice of interest kept by the protocol treasury). There is no other source of supplier yield in the base mechanism. [P] — [Aave V3 Overview](https://aave.com/docs/aave-v3/overview)
3. **Borrow.** A supplier can enable their deposit as collateral and borrow a different asset from the pool. "Positions are always over-collateralized, meaning the collateral value must exceed the borrowed amount." [P] — [Aave V3 Overview](https://aave.com/docs/aave-v3/overview). (There is no credit check, because borrowers are anonymous — [BIS Bulletin 57](https://www.bis.org/publ/bisbull57.htm). That loans are open-ended with no fixed repayment date is standard protocol design but is not quoted from a page I fetched; verify before stating.)
4. **Interest-rate curve.** "Interest rates adjust with utilization. v3 uses an interest rate model based on two slopes with an optimal utilization point: below the optimal point, borrow rates rise with the first slope; above it, they rise faster with the second slope." Utilisation = share of the pool currently lent out. Depict as a line chart with a "kink": gentle rise up to the optimal point, steep rise after it. [P] — [Aave V3 Overview](https://aave.com/docs/aave-v3/overview); [Aave Interest Rate Strategy docs](https://aave.com/docs/aave-v3/smart-contracts/interest-rate-strategy)
5. **Rate parameters are set by governance per asset and change.** Example: an August 2026 Aave risk-steward change on one deployment raised USDC optimal utilisation from 90% to 92% and cut Slope 2 from 40% to 20%. [P] — [Aave governance, IRM changes on Aave V3 Monad, 13 Aug 2026](https://governance.aave.com/t/risk-stewards-irm-changes-on-aave-v3-monad-2026-08-13/25475)
6. **Health factor.** "Health Factor = (Total Collateral Value * Weighted Average Liquidation Threshold) / Total Borrow Value." Aave's own example: "If you supply $10,000 in ETH with an 80% liquidation threshold and borrow $6,000 in GHO, your health factor would be 1.333." [P] — [Aave Help: Health factor and liquidations](https://aave.com/help/borrowing/liquidations)
7. **Liquidation trigger.** "A liquidation in the Aave Protocol occurs when a borrower's health factor falls below 1." [P] — [Aave Help: Health factor and liquidations](https://aave.com/help/borrowing/liquidations). (It follows from the formula that the health factor falls when collateral falls in price, when the borrowed asset rises in price, or as accrued interest grows the debt.)
8. **Who liquidates and what they get.** "Liquidations are permissionless, meaning any network participant can initiate the process." "A liquidator repays part of the debt and receives collateral at a discount (liquidation bonus)." [P] — [Aave Help](https://aave.com/help/borrowing/liquidations); [Aave V3 Overview](https://aave.com/docs/aave-v3/overview). (In practice liquidators are automated programs; that detail is general knowledge, not stated on the cited pages.)
9. **How much can be liquidated at once (close factor).** "Up to 50% of total debt can be liquidated when the health factor is above 0.95 AND both collateral and debt values are at least $2,000 each." "Up to 100% of debt can be liquidated when the health factor is 0.95 or below, OR when either collateral or debt value is below $2,000." [P] — [Aave Help](https://aave.com/help/borrowing/liquidations)
10. **Risk controls worth knowing.** Efficiency Mode (E-Mode) gives a "higher loan-to-value (LTV) ratio" for correlated assets; Isolation Mode means a listed asset "can be used as collateral to borrow only a specific basket of assets (typically stablecoins), up to a designated debt ceiling." [P] — [Aave V3 Overview](https://aave.com/docs/aave-v3/overview)

#### Worked numeric example of a liquidation (built on Aave's own published example and rules; the 5% bonus and the ETH prices are illustrative, not live parameters or price views)
- Start: Alice supplies 5 ETH when 1 ETH = $2,000, so collateral = $10,000. Liquidation threshold = 80%. She borrows $6,000 of a dollar stablecoin. Health factor = (10,000 x 0.80) / 6,000 = 1.333 (this is exactly Aave's published example). — [Aave Help](https://aave.com/help/borrowing/liquidations)
- Tipping point: HF = 1 when collateral value x 0.80 = 6,000, i.e. collateral = $7,500, i.e. ETH = $1,500 (a 25% fall). (Arithmetic from the formula above.)
- Price falls to $1,480: collateral = 5 x 1,480 = $7,400. HF = (7,400 x 0.80) / 6,000 = 0.987. Below 1, so the position can be liquidated. Because HF is above 0.95 and both sides exceed $2,000, a liquidator may repay up to 50% of the debt. — rule from [Aave Help](https://aave.com/help/borrowing/liquidations)
- Liquidation: a liquidator bot repays $3,000 of Alice's debt to the pool and takes $3,000 + 5% bonus = $3,150 of her ETH = 2.128 ETH (3,150 / 1,480).
- After: Alice has 2.872 ETH collateral (= $4,250) and $3,000 debt. HF = (4,250 x 0.80) / 3,000 = 1.133. She still has the $6,000 she borrowed. The liquidation itself cost her $150 (the bonus paid to the liquidator); the rest of her loss is the fall in the ETH price.
- What to show: suppliers are repaid by the liquidator's $3,000, not by Alice; the protocol never waits for Alice to act; Alice is not "margin called" by a person, a contract executes automatically.

#### (a) Well-established facts, safe to teach
- DeFi lending is over-collateralised because borrowers are anonymous: "Due to the anonymity of borrowers, overcollateralisation is pervasive in DeFi lending." [P] — [BIS Bulletin No 57, 14 June 2022](https://www.bis.org/publ/bisbull57.htm)
- Borrowing is mostly used for crypto speculation/leverage rather than real-economy credit: the BIS found the institutional features "mostly facilitate speculation in cryptoassets rather than real economy lending," and that over-collateralisation "limits access to credit to borrowers who are already asset-rich." (2022 analysis; I found no newer official study overturning it.) [P] — [BIS Bulletin No 57](https://www.bis.org/publ/bisbull57.htm)
- Collateral-based lending is procyclical: rising prices allow more borrowing, falling prices force liquidations that can deepen falls. [P] — [BIS Bulletin No 57](https://www.bis.org/publ/bisbull57.htm)
- Aave's cumulative liquidations: $4.65bn across more than 310,000 liquidation events from launch (2020) to 5 Feb 2026. [P, protocol's own blog] — [Aave blog, "How Aave Liquidations Perform Under Volatile Conditions", 6 Feb 2026](https://aave.com/blog/historical-liquidations)
- Largest Aave liquidation episodes per Aave: May 2021, $362m in 5,500+ liquidations; 10 Oct 2025, $250m+ in one day (average $68,000); 31 Jan to 5 Feb 2026, $429m in about 12,500 events (a record), "processed with no downtime or system failure." [P, self-reported] — [Aave blog, 6 Feb 2026](https://aave.com/blog/historical-liquidations)
- "Black Thursday", 12-13 March 2020 (MakerDAO, an over-collateralised stablecoin lender): ETH fell about 50%; network congestion stopped bidders; of 3,994 liquidation auctions, 1,462 (36.6%) were won by zero bids; $8.32m of users' locked collateral was lost to zero-bid auctions; the system recorded a shortfall reported as 6.65m DAI. [S] — [The Block, class-action report](https://www.theblock.co/post/61797/maker-foundation-class-action-lawsuit-black-thursday); [Cointelegraph](https://cointelegraph.com/features/makerdao-takes-new-measures-to-prevent-another-black-swan-collapse)
- Kelp rsETH incident, 18 April 2026 (primary account): at 17:35 UTC an attacker exploited Kelp's LayerZero bridge, configured as a "1-of-1 DVN" (a single verifier); a forged message released 116,500 rsETH with no matching burn. The attacker supplied 89,567 rsETH (about $221.4m) to Aave V3 as collateral and borrowed 82,650 WETH (about $190.9m) plus 821 wstETH (about $2.3m). "Aave's smart contracts were not compromised at any point during this event." Aave froze rsETH reserves about 19:00 UTC and later froze WETH on several deployments; WETH reserves on Ethereum, Arbitrum, Base, Linea and Mantle hit 100% utilisation (suppliers could not withdraw immediately). Modelled bad debt: $123.7m to $230.1m depending on loss allocation. [P] — [Aave governance, rsETH Incident Report, 20 April 2026](https://governance.aave.com/t/rseth-incident-report-april-20-2026/24580)
- Kelp aftermath: an industry coalition ("DeFi United") pledged ETH to restore rsETH backing; WETH loan-to-value settings were restored on Aave V3 on 17 May 2026; 106,993 rsETH was recovered via liquidations (89,567 on Aave V3, 17,426 on Compound). [S for the summary; P for the governance thread] — [Cointelegraph](https://cointelegraph.com/news/aave-restores-weth-borrowing-after-kelp-dao-rseth-exploit); [Aave governance, WETH unfreeze AIP](https://governance.aave.com/t/direct-to-aip-weth-unfreeze-and-ltv-restoration-across-aave-v3-instances/24878); [The Defiant](https://thedefiant.io/news/defi/defi-united-fundraising-chips-away-at-kelp-exploit-shortfall)
- Celsius (centralised lender): froze withdrawals in June 2022 and filed for Chapter 11 in July 2022, disclosing about $4.3bn of assets against $5.5bn of liabilities (a roughly $1.2bn hole). [S] — [Decrypt](https://decrypt.co/105136/celsius-bankruptcy-filing-1-2-billion-hole)

[S reporting a court filing] — [Decrypt on the examiner's report](https://decrypt.co/120266/celsius-problems-dated-back-least-2020-examiners-report)

#### (c) Contested or commonly misstated claims
- "Aave has $30bn+ / $50bn+ locked." Misleading without a definition. "Deposits" count everything supplied, including assets that are then lent out; DefiLlama's TVL nets out borrows. On 4 Oct 2026 the same protocol is about $32.5bn gross deposits and $19.3bn TVL. One blog claimed Aave TVL of $57.33bn in January 2026; DefiLlama's API shows $35.44bn on 15 Jan 2026 and a peak of $45.81bn (7 Oct 2025), so that figure does not match the primary dataset. — [DefiLlama API](https://api.llama.fi/protocol/aave); contradicted source: [stablecoininsider](https://stablecoininsider.org/aave-march-2026-report/)
- "$19bn of liquidations on 10 October 2025 shows DeFi lending failed." Inaccurate. The roughly $19bn figure refers to leveraged derivatives positions liquidated across trading venues; Aave's lending liquidations that day were $250m+ and were processed normally. [S for the $19bn; P for Aave] — [Aave blog](https://aave.com/blog/historical-liquidations); [Crowdfund Insider summary](https://www.crowdfundinsider.com/2026/02/262319-defi-protocols-aave-explains-how-liquidations-serve-as-essential-safeguards-protecting-lenders/). Confidence in the $19bn figure itself: medium (widely reported, not verified by me against a primary dataset).
- "Aave was hacked in April 2026." Inaccurate. Kelp's bridge was exploited; the stolen tokens were then used as collateral on Aave, leaving Aave with potential bad debt and frozen markets. Aave's report states its contracts "were not compromised." The accurate lesson: a lending pool is only as sound as the collateral it accepts. — [Aave governance incident report](https://governance.aave.com/t/rseth-incident-report-april-20-2026/24580)
- "Celsius was DeFi" / "DeFi lending collapsed in 2022." Inaccurate. Celsius, Voyager, BlockFi and Genesis were companies that took custody of customer assets and decided privately how to use them; the examiner found Celsius used customer assets to fund operations and rewards. — [Decrypt on examiner's report](https://decrypt.co/120266/celsius-problems-dated-back-least-2020-examiners-report)
- "You can withdraw from a lending pool at any time." Not guaranteed. Withdrawal needs unborrowed liquidity; at 100% utilisation (as in Aave's WETH markets in April 2026) suppliers must wait for repayments or liquidations. — [Aave governance incident report](https://governance.aave.com/t/rseth-incident-report-april-20-2026/24580)
- "Black Thursday shortfall" figures differ between sources (6.65m DAI in the reports above; other figures circulate). Teach the mechanism (congestion led to zero-bid auctions) rather than a single number unless a MakerDAO primary source is checked. — [Cointelegraph](https://cointelegraph.com/features/makerdao-takes-new-measures-to-prevent-another-black-swan-collapse)

### Inferences
- Standard form of the two-slope rate formula (high confidence it is correct, but the docs page I fetched described the two slopes without printing the formula, so verify before showing equations): below optimal utilisation, borrow rate = base + (U / U_optimal) x slope1; above it, borrow rate = base + slope1 + ((U - U_optimal) / (1 - U_optimal)) x slope2. Supplier rate is roughly borrow rate x utilisation x (1 - reserve factor), consistent with "supplier yields are funded by borrower interest net of the reserve factor."
- Because a borrower keeps the borrowed funds, liquidation is not a total loss of the position; the direct cost is the bonus paid to the liquidator plus being forced to sell collateral at a low price. This is the most commonly misunderstood point for beginners.
- The Kelp episode shows three distinct risks a lesson can separate: smart-contract risk in the lending protocol (did not fail), collateral/bridge risk (failed), and liquidity risk for suppliers (materialised).
- Aave's "no bad debt/no downtime" liquidation statistics are self-reported and predate April 2026; they should not be presented as proof that suppliers cannot lose money.

### Gaps
- No primary breakdown of who borrows (retail vs funds vs bots) or of loan purposes after 2022; the BIS 2022 bulletin is the best official source found. Claims about "looping" (re-depositing borrowed assets to build leverage) being a major share of borrowing are plausible but I did not find a primary quantified source.
- I did not fetch Compound's documentation; it uses the same pooled, over-collateralised, utilisation-based design, but that statement is unsourced here.
- Live per-asset parameters (LTV, liquidation threshold, liquidation bonus for ETH, USDC, etc.) were not captured; they change by governance vote. The 5% bonus in the worked example is illustrative.
- Final realised bad debt for Aave from the Kelp incident, and how it was allocated, was not confirmed from a primary source; news reports range from $177m to $236m "exposure" and describe a coalition bail-out rather than a final figure.
- DOJ press release on Mashinsky could not be read by the fetch tool; exact Celsius freeze date (widely reported as 12 June 2022) and Chapter 11 date (13 July 2022) should be confirmed from the docket. Exact Voyager dates differ by a day between sources.
- Primary dataset for the 10 October 2025 market-wide liquidation total not checked.

## 2. Staking: how Ethereum proof of stake works, how much is staked, yields, liquid staking and concentration

### Takeaway
Ethereum is secured by validators who lock at least 32 ETH, take turns proposing blocks every 12 seconds, vote ("attest") on blocks, and can lose stake for provable dishonesty; since the Pectra upgrade (7 May 2025) one validator can hold up to 2,048 ETH. On 4 Oct 2026 about 43.7 million ETH (about 35.8% of supply) was staked at roughly 2.6% APR, joining took about 26 days and leaving about 14 days, and one liquid-staking protocol, Lido, accounted for about 22.5% of all staked ETH.

### Cited Findings

#### Step-by-step mechanism (for the animator)
1. **Becoming a validator.** "To participate as a validator, a user must deposit 32 ETH into the deposit contract and run three separate pieces of software: an execution client, a consensus client, and a validator client. On depositing their ETH, the user joins an activation queue that limits the rate of new validators joining the network." [P] — [ethereum.org, Proof-of-stake (page updated 31 Aug 2026)](https://ethereum.org/en/developers/docs/consensus-mechanisms/pos/)
2. **Time is divided into slots and epochs.** "Time in proof-of-stake Ethereum is divided into slots (12 seconds) and epochs (32 slots)." So an epoch is 6.4 minutes. [P] — [ethereum.org, Proof-of-stake](https://ethereum.org/en/developers/docs/consensus-mechanisms/pos/)
3. **Proposing.** "One validator is randomly selected to be a block proposer in every slot" (pseudo-randomly, via a mechanism called RANDAO). [P] — [ethereum.org, Proof-of-stake](https://ethereum.org/en/developers/docs/consensus-mechanisms/pos/)
4. **Attesting.** "In every slot, a committee of validators is randomly chosen, whose votes are used to determine the validity of the block being proposed." "Committees divide up the validator set so that every active validator attests in every epoch, but not in every slot." [P] — [ethereum.org, Proof-of-stake](https://ethereum.org/en/developers/docs/consensus-mechanisms/pos/); [ethereum.org, Attestations](https://ethereum.org/en/developers/docs/consensus-mechanisms/pos/attestations/)
5. **Finality.** "The first block in each epoch is a checkpoint. Validators vote for pairs of checkpoints that it considers to be valid. If a pair of checkpoints attracts votes representing at least two-thirds of the total staked ETH, the checkpoints are upgraded." "The more recent of the two (target) becomes 'justified'. The earlier of the two is already justified because it was the 'target' in the previous epoch. Now it is upgraded to 'finalized'." "It takes about 15 minutes for an Ethereum block to finalize." "To revert a finalized block, an attacker would commit to losing at least one-third of the total supply of staked ETH." [P] — [ethereum.org, Proof-of-stake](https://ethereum.org/en/developers/docs/consensus-mechanisms/pos/); [ethereum.org, Single slot finality (updated 23 Jul 2026)](https://ethereum.org/en/roadmap/single-slot-finality/)
6. **Rewards.** A validator's base reward is proportional to its effective balance and inversely proportional to the square root of total staked ETH: `base_reward = effective_balance * (base_reward_factor / (base_rewards_per_epoch * sqrt(sum(active_balance))))`. So the more ETH is staked network-wide, the lower the rate each staker earns. Reward weights (out of 64): timely source vote 14, timely target vote 26, timely head vote 14, sync committee 2, block proposer 8. [P] — [ethereum.org, Rewards and penalties (updated 3 Apr 2026)](https://ethereum.org/en/developers/docs/consensus-mechanisms/pos/rewards-and-penalties/)
7. **Ordinary penalties (being offline).** "The penalties for missing the target and source votes are equal to the rewards the attestor would have received had they submitted them." There is no penalty for a missed head vote or a missed block proposal. Being offline costs roughly what would have been earned; it is not slashing. [P] — [ethereum.org, Rewards and penalties](https://ethereum.org/en/developers/docs/consensus-mechanisms/pos/rewards-and-penalties/)
8. **Slashing (provable dishonesty).** "Two primary behaviors can be considered dishonest: proposing multiple blocks in a single slot (equivocating) and submitting contradictory attestations." On slashing, "0.0078125 is immediately burned for a 32 ETH validator (scaled linearly with active balance)" (that is 1/4096 of the balance); a 36-day removal period begins; "at the mid-point (Day 18) an additional penalty is applied whose magnitude scales with the total staked ether of all slashed validators in the 36 days prior." A lone accident costs little; a coordinated attack can cost the whole stake. [P] — [ethereum.org, Proof-of-stake](https://ethereum.org/en/developers/docs/consensus-mechanisms/pos/); [ethereum.org, Rewards and penalties](https://ethereum.org/en/developers/docs/consensus-mechanisms/pos/rewards-and-penalties/)
9. **Inactivity leak.** If the chain fails to finalize for more than four epochs, "the inactivity leak bleeds away the staked ETH from validators voting against the majority, allowing the majority to regain a two-thirds majority and finalize the chain." [P] — [ethereum.org, Proof-of-stake](https://ethereum.org/en/developers/docs/consensus-mechanisms/pos/)
10. **Getting rewards out.** For legacy ("0x01", Type 1) validators, "excess balance over 32 ETH is automatically and regularly sent to the withdrawal address"; no transaction or gas is needed. Compounding ("0x02", Type 2) validators keep rewards staked up to 2,048 ETH. The automatic "sweep" processes "a maximum of 16 withdrawals... in a single block." [P] — [ethereum.org, Staking withdrawals (updated 17 Aug 2026)](https://ethereum.org/en/staking/withdrawals/)
11. **Leaving.** A validator signals exit, waits in the exit queue (rate-limited), then its full balance is paid out by the sweep. Since Pectra, an exit can also be triggered from the withdrawal address via a contract call, without the validator signing key (EIP-7002). [P] — [ethereum.org, Staking withdrawals](https://ethereum.org/en/staking/withdrawals/); [ethereum.org, Pectra](https://ethereum.org/en/roadmap/pectra/)

#### (a) Well-established facts, safe to teach
- Pectra activated on Ethereum mainnet on 7 May 2025 at 10:05 UTC (epoch 364032). "A single validator can now stake between 32 and 2048 ETH." 32 ETH remains the minimum to activate a validator. [P] — [ethereum.org, Pectra](https://ethereum.org/en/roadmap/pectra/)
- EIP-7251 (status: Final) raised MAX_EFFECTIVE_BALANCE from 32 ETH to 2,048 ETH, kept a 32 ETH MIN_ACTIVATION_BALANCE, let operators consolidate many validators into fewer, switched queue rate-limits from validator count to stake weight, and made "the initial slashing penalty negligible." [P] — [EIP-7251](https://eips.ethereum.org/EIPS/eip-7251)
- Staked ETH could not be withdrawn at all until 2023: "Withdrawal functionality was originally enabled as part of the Shanghai/Capella upgrade on April 12, 2023." [P] — [ethereum.org, Staking withdrawals](https://ethereum.org/en/staking/withdrawals/)
- Liquid staking (Lido as the example): users deposit ETH into a pooled protocol, professional "node operators" run the validators, and the user receives a transferable token (stETH) representing the staked ETH that "can be used across a range of DeFi applications." "Lido applies a 10% fee on staking rewards that are split between node operators and the DAO Treasury." [P] — [Lido docs](https://docs.lido.fi/)
- Single-slot finality is not live: "SSF is in the research phase." [P] — [ethereum.org, Single slot finality](https://ethereum.org/en/roadmap/single-slot-finality/)

## 3. UK treatment of staking: regulatory status and HMRC tax treatment

### Takeaway
In the UK, staking is legal, is expressly not a collective investment scheme (since 31 January 2025), and becomes a specifically FCA-regulated activity when the new cryptoasset regime starts on 25 October 2027; final FCA rules were published on 30 June 2026. For tax, HMRC treats staking rewards received by an individual who is not trading as miscellaneous income valued in pounds at receipt, with Capital Gains Tax on later disposal; proposed "no gain, no loss" rules for crypto lending and liquidity pools (effective 6 April 2027) were published in draft on 13 July 2026 and are not yet law.

### Cited Findings

#### (a) Well-established facts, safe to teach
- The Financial Services and Markets Act 2000 (Collective Investment Schemes) (Amendment) Order 2025 (SI 2025/17), made 8 January 2025, in force 31 January 2025, provides that "arrangements for qualifying cryptoasset staking do not amount to a collective investment scheme," defining it as "the use of a qualifying cryptoasset in blockchain validation." [P] — [legislation.gov.uk, SI 2025/17](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/uksi/2025/17/made)
- The new UK cryptoasset regime (including staking as a regulated activity) takes effect on 25 October 2027. The application window for firms opens 30 September 2026 and closes 28 February 2027. [P] — [FCA, overview of cryptoasset regime policy statements](https://www.fca.org.uk/publications/policy-statements/cryptoasset-regime)
- FCA Policy Statement PS26/11 "Regulated Cryptoasset Activities" (30 June 2026) sets final conduct rules for trading platforms, intermediaries, lending and borrowing, staking and safeguarding. The FCA summarises the retail protections as "enhanced disclosures, consent, appropriateness testing, record-keeping, and safeguards such as over-collateralisation and negative balance protection," and allows "consent to cover ongoing staking of current and future holdings subject to conditions and annual notification." [P] — [FCA overview page](https://www.fca.org.uk/publications/policy-statements/cryptoasset-regime); full text: [PS26/11 PDF](https://www.fca.org.uk/publication/policy/ps26-11.pdf)
- Regulated "qualifying cryptoasset staking" is framed as making arrangements for the use of qualifying cryptoassets in blockchain validation (i.e. it targets firms that stake on customers' behalf, not the protocol itself). [S, law-firm summaries] — [DWF, July 2026](https://dwfgroup.com/en/news-and-insights/insights/2026/7/the-fcas-new-cryptoasset-regime-explained); [A&O Shearman](https://www.aoshearman.com/en/insights/ao-shearman-on-fintech-and-digital-assets/final-rules-for-new-uk-crypto-regime-regulated-cryptoasset-activities)
- On DeFi, the FCA "maintains that it will apply rules where there is an identifiable controlling person, taking a case-by-case approach," with further DeFi guidance to be consulted on. [S] — [A&O Shearman](https://www.aoshearman.com/en/insights/ao-shearman-on-fintech-and-digital-assets/final-rules-for-new-uk-crypto-regime-regulated-cryptoasset-activities)
- HMRC, staking by individuals (page updated 28 November 2025): whether staking amounts to a trade depends on "degree of activity, organisation, risk, commerciality." If it is not a trade, "the pound sterling value (at the time of receipt) of any tokens awarded will be taxable as income (miscellaneous income)," with appropriate expenses deductible, and "If the individual keeps the awarded assets, they may have to pay Capital Gains Tax when they later dispose of them." [P] — [HMRC Cryptoassets Manual CRYPTO21200](https://www.gov.uk/hmrc-internal-manuals/cryptoassets-manual/crypto21200)
- HMRC, DeFi lending and "staking" (liquidity provision) — capital gains (updated 28 November 2025): "Where the making of a loan/staking results in the lender/liquidity provider transferring their beneficial ownership of the tokens to the borrower/DeFi lending platform, this will give rise to a disposal of the loaned/staked tokens." The test is whether the recipient can "deal with the tokens received as they want." [P] — [HMRC CRYPTO61620](https://www.gov.uk/hmrc-internal-manuals/cryptoassets-manual/crypto61620)
- HMRC, nature of the DeFi return (updated 28 November 2025): the return may be income or capital depending on the facts; factors include whether the return is known in advance, whether it is paid periodically, and the lending period. Income-like returns are compared to interest: "the return is the 'interest or fruit' while the tokens are the 'principal or tree'." [P] — [HMRC CRYPTO61214](https://www.gov.uk/hmrc-internal-manuals/cryptoassets-manual/crypto61214)

#### (b) Fast-changing items (date-stamp them)
- Proposed reform, published 13 July 2026: a policy paper and draft legislation would treat (1) single-cryptoasset lending arrangements, (2) single-cryptoasset borrowing arrangements and (3) automated market making (liquidity pool) arrangements on a "no gain, no loss" basis for CGT. "This measure will have effect from 6 April 2027," to be legislated in Finance Bill 2026-27. Status on 4 Oct 2026: draft, not enacted. [P] — [GOV.UK, Tax treatment of cryptoasset loans and liquidity pools](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/cryptoasset-loans-and-liquidity-pools/tax-treatment-of-cryptoasset-loans-and-liquidity-pools); [draft legislation PDF](https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/media/6a50e1b6a4890e65cce64cd9/Cryptoassets_and_liquidity_pools_draft_legislation.pdf); commentary: [KPMG](https://kpmg.com/uk/en/insights/tax/tmd-taxation-of-crypto-loans-liquidity-pools-stablecoins.html)
- The FCA regime is not yet in force: until 25 October 2027, firms offering staking to UK customers are covered by existing rules (anti-money-laundering registration and the financial promotions regime), not by the new conduct rules. [P for the date; S for the description of the interim position] — [FCA overview page](https://www.fca.org.uk/publications/policy-statements/cryptoasset-regime); [Skadden, Jan 2025](https://skadden.com/insights/publications/2025/01/cryptoasset-staking-excluded-from-the-uk-fund-compliance-regime)

#### (c) Contested or commonly misstated claims
- "Staking is unregulated in the UK" / "staking is FCA-regulated." Both are imprecise in October 2026. Accurate version: staking is lawful; it is expressly excluded from the collective-investment-scheme rules; firms' marketing is subject to the financial promotions regime; staking as a specifically authorised activity starts on 25 October 2027. — [SI 2025/17](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/uksi/2025/17/made); [FCA overview](https://www.fca.org.uk/publications/policy-statements/cryptoasset-regime)
- "Staking rewards are tax-free until you sell." Wrong under HMRC guidance: rewards are income when received (valued in pounds at that time), and a later disposal can also give rise to CGT. — [CRYPTO21200](https://www.gov.uk/hmrc-internal-manuals/cryptoassets-manual/crypto21200)
- "The UK has scrapped tax on DeFi lending and staking." Overstated. The July 2026 measure is draft, starts 6 April 2027 if enacted, and removes the CGT disposal on entering/leaving qualifying lending, borrowing and liquidity-pool arrangements; it does not make the returns tax-free, and the policy paper's three scenarios do not mention proof-of-stake validator rewards. — [GOV.UK policy paper](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/cryptoasset-loans-and-liquidity-pools/tax-treatment-of-cryptoasset-loans-and-liquidity-pools)
- HMRC uses "staking" for two different things: validator staking on a proof-of-stake network (CRYPTO21200) and DeFi "staking", meaning liquidity provision/lending (CRYPTO61000 series). Lessons should keep them apart. — [CRYPTO21200](https://www.gov.uk/hmrc-internal-manuals/cryptoassets-manual/crypto21200); [CRYPTO61000 contents](https://www.gov.uk/hmrc-internal-manuals/cryptoassets-manual/crypto61000)
- HMRC manuals are guidance, not law; HMRC states treatment depends on the facts of each case (trade versus miscellaneous income; income versus capital). — [CRYPTO61214](https://www.gov.uk/hmrc-internal-manuals/cryptoassets-manual/crypto61214)

### Inferences
- Under current guidance, depositing tokens into a DeFi pool can be a taxable disposal even though the user thinks of it as "just lending"; that "dry tax charge" is the problem the July 2026 draft addresses.
- Swapping ETH for a liquid-staking token is likely to be analysed as a token-for-token exchange (a disposal) or under the beneficial-ownership test in CRYPTO61620; I did not find an HMRC page that addresses liquid-staking tokens by name, so do not teach a firm rule.
- The site can safely say "from 25 October 2027 firms that arrange staking for UK customers will need FCA authorisation," citing the FCA page, but should avoid describing detailed duties (e.g. compensation for slashing) until PS26/11 itself has been read.

### Gaps
- The PS26/11 PDF is encrypted and could not be parsed by my tools; the detailed staking rules (disclosures, liability for firm-caused losses, safeguarding of staked assets) rest on the FCA summary page and law-firm summaries. Someone should read PS26/11 directly before those details are taught.
- I did not fetch the statutory instrument that creates the new regulated activities (the Financial Services and Markets Act 2000 (Cryptoassets) Regulations) to confirm the statutory definition of staking.
- Not checked: the £1,000 trading and miscellaneous income allowance, CGT annual exempt amount and rates for 2026-27, and Cryptoasset Reporting Framework (CARF) reporting by platforms from 2026; all are relevant to a UK staking-tax lesson and need HMRC citations.
- Whether the 2026 draft legislation changes the income-tax characterisation of lending returns was not established.

Size and maturity not stated. No DIGIT has been issued as of 4 Oct 2026. [P] — [GOV.UK, Update on the DIGIT pilot issuance](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/update-on-the-digital-gilt-instrument-digit-pilot-issuance/update-on-the-digital-gilt-instrument-digit-pilot-issuance)

Still a pilot, not a product the public can open. [P, trade body] — [UK Finance, Tokenised sterling deposits](https://www.ukfinance.org.uk/tokenised-sterling-deposits)
- Tokenised stocks access: reported as available to EU/EEA customers (Robinhood) and non-US customers (xStocks via Backed/Kraken), with US access limited; a US SEC "innovation exemption" order for trading tokenised stocks was reported for 17 September (2026). [S, low confidence; not verified with SEC or issuer documents] — [eco.com explainer](https://eco.com/support/en/articles/15083160-robinhood-tokenized-stocks-what-s-live-and-how-it-works); [Webull news item](https://www.webull.com/news/15616395257332736)

#### (c) Contested or commonly misstated claims
- "Trillions of dollars of assets are already tokenised." Wrong. The measured, transferable total excluding stablecoins was $38.73bn on 4 Oct 2026. The much larger "represented" figure ($388.6bn) covers assets whose records sit on a ledger but which, per rwa.xyz's categorisation, are not freely transferable tokens; do not present it as the size of the tradable market. Multi-trillion figures are forecasts, not measurements. — [rwa.xyz](https://app.rwa.xyz/)
- "Anyone can buy tokenised Treasuries." Mostly wrong. BUIDL is reported as needing qualified-purchaser status and $5m; summaries of IOSCO's report say secondary trading of tokenised money-market funds is limited to whitelisted investors. Some products target retail outside the US, but eligibility is product- and country-specific. [S] — [Crane Data on BUIDL](https://www.cranedata.com/archives/all-articles/10236); [Regulation Tomorrow on IOSCO](https://www.regulationtomorrow.com/2025/11/iosco-final-report-on-the-tokenisation-of-financial-assets/)
- "The token is the asset." Imprecise. For fund tokens the token is a record of a fund share, which the BIS describes as a security; the Treasuries are held by the fund, off-chain. The holder's rights depend on the legal structure; IOSCO lists "legal uncertainty" among the risks. Whether a given "tokenised stock" carries shareholder rights varies by product and was not verified here. — [BIS Bulletin 115](https://www.bis.org/publ/bisbull115.htm); [IOSCO release via Mondo Visione](https://mondovisione.com/media-and-resources/news/iosco-publishes-final-report-on-financial-asset-tokenization-20251111/)
- "Tokenised Treasuries are the same as stablecoins." No. The BIS says tokenised money-market funds, "like stablecoins... circulate on public permissionless blockchains but offer returns at money market rates and regulatory protections as securities." They are linked: the BIS calls them "a fast-growing collateral asset and savings instrument in the crypto ecosystem." — [BIS Bulletin 115](https://www.bis.org/publ/bisbull115.htm)
- "The UK has issued a digital gilt." Not yet. First issuance is planned "by Q1 2027." — [GOV.UK DIGIT update, 16 Jul 2026](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/update-on-the-digital-gilt-instrument-digit-pilot-issuance/update-on-the-digital-gilt-instrument-digit-pilot-issuance)
- "Tokenised deposits are a kind of stablecoin." No. They are commercial bank deposits in token form, issued by the bank to its own customers, which UK Finance says "retain the trust and regulatory protections of conventional deposits." — [UK Finance](https://www.ukfinance.org.uk/tokenised-sterling-deposits)
- Market-size figures differ by source and date: news summaries cited "$22-25bn" (May 2026) for RWAs and "$10bn" to "$13bn" for tokenised Treasuries; these were snapshots with different inclusion rules. Use rwa.xyz directly with a date. — [rwa.xyz](https://app.rwa.xyz/)

### Inferences
- Live today: tokenised money-market/Treasury funds, tokenised gold, some tokenised credit and stock tokens, institutional deposit tokens (JPMD), UK authorised-fund tokenisation rules, two DSS firms with live permission. Pilot or planned: DIGIT (by Q1 2027), GBTD (pilot phases), most DSS entrants (Gate 1 testing), multi-bank deposit-token networks.
- The tokenised Treasury segment shrank 6.6% in the 30 days to 4 Oct 2026, so "always growing" is not a safe statement.
- BUIDL was the largest product in 2025 commentary but ranks third on 4 Oct 2026 behind Circle's USYC and Ondo's USDY; rankings change and should not be hard-coded into lessons.
- For a UK beginner audience the honest framing is that most tokenised RWAs are currently institutional plumbing rather than something a UK retail user can buy; the retail-relevant UK developments are fund tokenisation (PS26/7) and, later, DIGIT's listing.

### Gaps
- rwa.xyz's exact definitions of "distributed" versus "represented" value were not fetched; confirm on its documentation before explaining the difference.
- UK retail eligibility for any specific tokenised Treasury or stock product was not established; FCA financial-promotion and distribution rules would govern this. The issuer and investor eligibility of the UKTBL product were not checked.
- Whether tokenised sterling deposits carry FSCS protection is not stated explicitly on the UK Finance page ("regulatory protections of conventional deposits" is the only wording found); a Bank of England/PRA statement should be sourced.
- The SEC "innovation exemption" (September 2026) and current US/EU eligibility for tokenised stocks rest on low-quality secondary sources.
- I did not retrieve FSB, IMF or Bank of England Financial Policy Committee publications on tokenisation; BIS and IOSCO are the official sources used. The BIS Annual Economic Report 2026 chapter ["Anchoring trust in money: innovation beyond stablecoins"](https://www.bis.org/publ/arpdf/ar2026e3.htm) appeared in results but was not read.
- BUIDL's current share classes, fee and chain list were not confirmed from the Securitize page (it returned little content).

## 5. Risk: losses to hacks and exploits per year, main causes, notable smart-contract and bridge failures to 2026

### Takeaway
Thefts run at roughly $1.5bn to $3.5bn a year depending on who is counting: Chainalysis reports $2.2bn stolen in 2024 and over $3.4bn in 2025, and DefiLlama's incident database shows about $2.24bn already in 2026 to 1 October. The dominant causes are compromised keys and signing infrastructure, social engineering of the people who control funds, and cross-chain bridge flaws, with pure smart-contract coding bugs a smaller share of losses than commonly assumed; a few very large incidents dominate each year's total.

### Cited Findings

#### (a) Well-established facts, safe to teach
- 2025 (Chainalysis, published 18 Dec 2025): over $3.4bn stolen; the Bybit hack of February 2025 alone was $1.5bn; North Korea-linked actors stole $2.02bn (up 51% year on year; $6.75bn all-time); "the top three hacks in 2025 account for 69% of all service losses"; personal wallet compromises were $713m (20% of the total) across 158,000 incidents and at least 80,000 victims. [P] — [Chainalysis, 2025 crypto theft report](https://www.chainalysis.com/blog/crypto-hacking-stolen-funds-2026/)
- 2024 (Chainalysis, published 19 Dec 2024): $2.2bn stolen across 303 incidents (up about 21% on 2023); private key compromises accounted for 43.8% of stolen funds; largest: DMM Bitcoin $305m (May 2024) and WazirX $234.9m (July 2024); North Korea-linked $1.34bn across 47 incidents. [P] — [Chainalysis, 2024 crypto theft report](https://www.chainalysis.com/blog/crypto-hacking-stolen-funds-2025/)
- DefiLlama incident database, yearly totals (my aggregation of the API on 4 Oct 2026; amounts as recorded, not net of recoveries): 2021 $2.87bn (127 incidents); 2022 $3.63bn (184); 2023 $1.63bn (197); 2024 $1.67bn (217); 2025 $2.72bn (148); 2026 to 1 Oct $2.24bn (286). All-time recorded: $21.1bn, of which bridge incidents $3.69bn across 96 incidents. [P, computed] — [DefiLlama hacks API](https://api.llama.fi/hacks); dashboard: [DefiLlama Hacks](https://defillama.com/hacks)
- Largest recorded incidents (DefiLlama classifications): Bybit, Feb 2025, $1.4bn, signer phishing/social engineering; Ronin Bridge, Mar 2022, $624m, validator keys compromised; Poly Network, Aug 2021, $611m, improper access control (bridge); BNB/Binance Bridge, Oct 2022, $570m, proof-verifier bug; FTX, Nov 2022, $450m; Wormhole ("Portal"), Feb 2022, $326m, signature-verification flaw (bridge); DMM Bitcoin, May 2024, $305m; Cetus (Sui exchange), May 2025, $223m, arithmetic error; Balancer V2, Nov 2025, $128m, rounding error. [P] — [DefiLlama hacks API](https://api.llama.fi/hacks)
- Bridges are a recurring weak point because they hold large pooled collateral and rely on a small set of verifiers or keys: Ronin (keys), Wormhole (signature check), BNB Bridge (proof verifier), Kelp 2026 (single verifier), Liquid 2026 (proof validation) are all in the top incidents. [P] — [DefiLlama hacks API](https://api.llama.fi/hacks)

[S] — [The Block](https://theblock.co/news/markets/2026-09-24-more-than-170-million-in-crypto-moves-from-bitget-wallets-unidentified-address-416345); exchange notice: [Bitget support](https://www.bitget.com/support/articles/12560603896024)

#### (c) Contested or commonly misstated claims
- "X billion was stolen in year Y" has no single correct value. For 2025, Chainalysis says over $3.4bn while DefiLlama's database sums to $2.72bn; for 2024, $2.2bn versus $1.67bn. Differences come from scope (Chainalysis includes personal-wallet thefts), valuation date, and which incidents are included. Always name the source. — [Chainalysis 2025](https://www.chainalysis.com/blog/crypto-hacking-stolen-funds-2026/); [DefiLlama hacks API](https://api.llama.fi/hacks)
- Bybit is "$1.5bn" (Chainalysis) or "$1.4bn" (DefiLlama): same incident, different valuation. — same sources as above.
- "Most losses come from smart-contract bugs." Not supported by recent data. In 2024, 43.8% of stolen value came from private-key compromises (Chainalysis); in 2025 the largest loss was a signer-phishing attack on a centralised exchange; in 2026 to date DefiLlama's largest categories are key compromise and bridge failures, and the Drift and Bitget losses were operational/social-engineering rather than code bugs. Contract bugs (Cetus, Balancer) remain significant but are not the majority by value. — [Chainalysis 2024](https://www.chainalysis.com/blog/crypto-hacking-stolen-funds-2025/); [DefiLlama hacks API](https://api.llama.fi/hacks)
- "Hacks are falling" versus "hacks are at record highs." Both appear in 2026 coverage: H1 2026 dollar losses were lower than H1 2025 (because of Bybit) while incident counts were a record; then September 2026 alone added about $742m. — [ForkLog on CertiK](https://forklog.com/en/certik-says-crypto-hack-losses-hit-1-32b-in-h1-2026/); [DefiLlama hacks API](https://api.llama.fi/hacks)
- "Stolen" does not always mean permanently lost: Poly Network's and most of Liquid's funds were returned, and DefiLlama records Wormhole's $326m as made good. Headline totals are gross. — [DefiLlama hacks API](https://api.llama.fi/hacks); [Security Affairs on Liquid](https://securityaffairs.com/198697/cyber-crime/hackers-drain-320-million-from-liquid-network-then-return-most-of-it.html)
- DefiLlama's 2020 total ($3.98bn) is inflated by one retrospectively added entry (LuBian mining pool, $3.5bn, dated December 2020, classified as weak key generation); do not present 2020 as a peak year without that caveat. — [DefiLlama hacks API](https://api.llama.fi/hacks)
- "Audited means safe" and "hacks only affect DeFi." Neither holds: Bybit and Bitget are centralised exchanges; Balancer V2 and Cetus were established, audited protocols per the incident record. (Audit status itself not verified here.) — [DefiLlama hacks API](https://api.llama.fi/hacks)

### Inferences
- A beginner-level, accurate summary of causes in priority order: (1) stolen or misused keys and signing systems, (2) tricking the humans who approve transactions, (3) bridges between blockchains, (4) bugs in contract arithmetic/logic, (5) price-oracle manipulation.
- Losses are extremely concentrated: one incident was about 44% of Chainalysis's 2025 total; four incidents (Bitget, Liquid, Drift, Kelp) are about 58% of DefiLlama's 2026 year-to-date total ($1.295bn of $2.24bn).
- 2026's incident count (286 to 1 October) already exceeds every prior full year in DefiLlama's data; this may partly reflect broader data collection rather than only more attacks. Medium-low confidence in the trend interpretation.
- Lending, staking and tokenisation lessons can each point to a matching real incident: lending and collateral risk (Kelp/Aave 2026), liquid/re-staking token risk (Kelp), custodial exchange risk (Bybit 2025, Bitget 2026), bridge risk (Ronin, Wormhole, Liquid).

### Gaps
- No Chainalysis mid-year 2026 or year-to-date 2026 theft total was found; 2026 totals rest on DefiLlama's dataset and on secondary reports of CertiK/TRM/Immunefi. The primary CertiK, TRM Labs and Immunefi H1 2026 reports were not retrieved.
- Tectonic and COLDCARD 2026 entries were not corroborated. Bitget and Liquid details come from news/security-firm write-ups, not final post-mortems.
- Older landmark failures (The DAO 2016, Parity 2017, Nomad 2022, Euler 2023, Curve 2023) were not individually researched or sourced here; use the DefiLlama dataset or primary post-mortems before teaching them.
- Recovery rates (how much is ultimately returned or frozen each year) were not quantified.
- Tokenised-RWA-specific losses (e.g. issuer or custodian failure of a tokenised fund) were not found; absence of evidence here is not evidence that the risk is nil.
