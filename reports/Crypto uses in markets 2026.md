# Crypto trades more than it pays

As of October 2026 crypto is used mainly to trade and to hold, and only marginally to pay: derivatives made up **79.3% of centralised-exchange volume in August 2026** ([CoinDesk Data](https://www.coindesk.com/research/exchange-review-august-2026)), half of UK stablecoin buyers bought them in order to trade other crypto, and only **17% of UK crypto owners have ever bought goods or services with it** ([FCA Wave 6](https://www.fca.org.uk/publication/research-notes/cryptoasset-consumer-research-2025-wave-6.pdf)). That matters for EndorseCrypto because the ten planned lessons describe mechanisms (blocks, keys, payments, supply, stablecoins, exchanges, pools, staking, lending, tokenised assets) that the research sources to primary documentation, while almost every number about how large or popular those things are is a snapshot that goes stale within months. The UK frame is stark: **8% of UK adults held crypto** in the FCA's 2025 survey, down from 12%; the FCA still calls crypto "largely unregulated"; the full authorisation regime starts on **25 October 2027**; and there is no Financial Services Compensation Scheme cover now or planned ([FCA Wave 6](https://www.fca.org.uk/publication/research-notes/cryptoasset-consumer-research-2025-wave-6.pdf); [FCA](https://www.fca.org.uk/consumers/cryptoassets); [FCA PS26/13](https://www.fca.org.uk/publication/policy/ps26-13.pdf)). All ten lessons can be written from sources the researchers opened and read, provided each lesson states the mechanism plainly, dates every figure, and replaces a set of popular claims (stablecoins are "cash in a bank", blockchains are "immutable", Aave "was hacked", "12% of UK adults own crypto") with the sourced versions set out below. A further group of findings was seen only as search summaries or second-hand reports, including the official records for FTX, Mt. Gox, QuadrigaCX and Celsius, the McKinsey estimate of real stablecoin payments, and the terms of BlackRock's tokenised fund; under the site's rule these stay out of lessons until someone opens the primary page. Scams and UK tax are thoroughly covered by the research but are not among the ten lessons, which leaves a gap, because swaps, staking and lending are all actions HMRC treats as taxable.

## Four labels sort every finding: safe, dated, corrected, hold

The six research notes tag each finding twice: by type of claim and by how it was verified. This report collapses those tags into four labels. **Safe** means a mechanism or settled fact that a researcher read in a primary source (protocol documentation, legislation, a regulator's page, an agency release) and that does not change month to month. **Dated** means true on a stated date and wrong soon after; it is usable only with its "as of" date and source beside it. **Corrected** means a popular claim the sources contradict, paired with the accurate version. **Hold** means the researchers saw the claim only in a search-result summary or a second-hand report, could not open the primary page, or found sources that disagree; it is not taught until someone opens the source. Live figures were read on 4 October 2026 unless another date is given, and figures described as "computed" are the researchers' own arithmetic from public data.

Two cautions apply to all four labels. A primary source is not automatically a current one: the Bitcoin developer guide still describes a 1 MB block limit and a 1,000-satoshi minimum fee, both superseded ([BIP-141](https://github.com/bitcoin/bips/blob/master/bip-0141.mediawiki); [Bitcoin Core 30.0 release notes](https://bitcoincore.org/en/releases/30.0/)), so "sourced" must also mean "checked for currency". And the notes sometimes disagree with each other on live numbers because they used different trackers; where that happens this report gives both readings and names both sources.

## Markets in 2026: most activity is trading, and four-fifths of exchange volume is derivatives

The notes describe a market whose plumbing is sophisticated and whose main use is still speculation. The FCA says it uses "cryptoasset" rather than "cryptocurrency" "to reflect their current primary use as a speculative investment" ([FCA Wave 6](https://www.fca.org.uk/publication/research-notes/cryptoasset-consumer-research-2025-wave-6.pdf)), and the BIS found that DeFi lending's features "mostly facilitate speculation in cryptoassets rather than real economy lending" ([BIS Bulletin 57](https://www.bis.org/publ/bisbull57.htm)). Each lesson topic below is real and measurable. None is yet an everyday payment or savings tool for a UK adult.

### Stablecoins are roughly $300bn of trading collateral with a thin payments layer

The safe core of a stablecoin lesson is the two-market structure. Issuers mint and burn tokens only with approved customers, which the Federal Reserve describes as "a set of approved customers that tend to be companies rather than retail traders"; everyone else buys existing tokens on exchanges at a market price ([Federal Reserve](https://www.federalreserve.gov/econres/notes/feds-notes/primary-and-secondary-markets-for-stablecoins-20240223.html)). Circle's minting service "is for institutional customers" ([Circle](https://www.circle.com/circle-mint)), and Tether sets a **$100,000 minimum**, a redemption fee of the greater of $1,000 or 0.1%, and sole discretion over who gets an account ([Tether](https://tether.to/en/fees/)). Reserves are not cash in a bank: most USDC backing sits in a BlackRock-managed government money market fund holding short-dated Treasuries and overnight repo ([Circle](https://www.circle.com/transparency)), and Tether's quarterly report is an attestation by BDO, not an audit ([Tether](https://tether.io/news/tether-posts-strong-q2-performance-generates-1-5b-net-operating-profit-maintains-4-11b-reserve-buffer-and-expands-gold-holdings-to-more-than-146-tons/)).

Three designs behave differently and fail differently, and all three are safe to teach. Fiat-backed coins (USDC, USDT) depend on reserves and on banks being open. Crypto-collateralised coins such as DAI are created by borrowers who lock collateral worth more than they mint, so that "every Dai in circulation is directly backed by excess collateral" ([Maker whitepaper](https://makerdao.com/en/whitepaper/)). Algorithmic coins had no reserve at all: TerraUSD relied on a swap rule with its sister token, collapsed in May 2022 with "over $40 billion" in losses, and its founder was sentenced to 15 years in December 2025 after admitting fraud ([US DOJ](https://www.justice.gov/usao-sdny/pr/crypto-enabled-fraudster-sentenced-orchestrating-40-billion-fraud)). The second safe failure story is USDC in March 2023: **$3.3bn of roughly $40bn** in reserves was stuck at Silicon Valley Bank, the price fell below 90 cents over a weekend when redemptions were closed, and it recovered once US authorities protected the bank's depositors ([Federal Reserve](https://www.federalreserve.gov/econres/notes/feds-notes/primary-and-secondary-markets-for-stablecoins-20240223.html); [joint statement](https://www.federalreserve.gov/newsevents/pressreleases/monetary20230312b.htm)). A third, newer type, Ethena's USDe, is by its issuer's own statement "not the same as a fiat stablecoin" ([Ethena](https://docs.ethena.fi/)); on 10 October 2025 it traded near $0.65 on one exchange for about 40 minutes while staying close to $1 elsewhere ([21Shares](https://www.21shares.com/research/why-did-ethenas-stablecoin-remain-stable-onchain-but-depegged-on-binance)).

What the coins are used for is equally well sourced. The BIS says stablecoins serve "as an on- and off-ramp to the crypto ecosystem" and that over 99% are dollar-denominated ([BIS](https://www.bis.org/publ/arpdf/ar2025e3.htm)). Visa's own analysis cut a $3.9tn month of raw transfers to $817.5bn once bot-like activity was removed and found retail-sized transactions were **under 1% of even the adjusted figure** ([Visa](https://corporate.visa.com/en/sites/visa-perspectives/trends-insights/making-sense-of-stablecoins.html)). In the UK the FCA found consumer uptake "low and limited to overseas issuers", with no issuer operating from the UK ([FCA PS26/10](https://www.fca.org.uk/publication/policy/ps26-10.pdf)). Payment use exists but sits mostly behind the scenes. Stripe's stablecoin checkout pays the merchant in local currency, has no chargebacks, and is open to US businesses only, with the UK not listed ([Stripe](https://docs.stripe.com/payments/stablecoin-payments)). PayPal has let UK users buy, hold and send its PYUSD coin since 17 March 2026, without rewards ([PayPal](https://newsroom.paypal-corp.com/2026-03-17-PAYPAL-BRINGS-PAYPAL-USD-TO-USERS-ACROSS-70-MARKETS)). Any "cheaper remittances" claim has a benchmark to beat and no data behind it: the World Bank put digital-only operators at 3.55% for a $200 transfer in Q1 2025, and its survey does not measure stablecoin routes at all ([World Bank](https://remittanceprices.worldbank.org/sites/default/files/rpw_main_report_and_annex_q125_1_0.pdf)).

The dated figures are these. DefiLlama tracked **$315.76bn** of stablecoins on 4 October 2026, with USDT at $184.03bn (58.3%) and USDC at $74.21bn (23.5%), a total that includes yield-bearing fund tokens ([DefiLlama](https://stablecoins.llama.fi/stablecoins?includePrices=true)); rwa.xyz showed $295.04bn the same day ([rwa.xyz](https://app.rwa.xyz/)). The $20bn gap is definitional, and that is the teaching point: a market size without a tracker's name and a date is not a fact. Tether reported $187.75bn of assets against $183.64bn of liabilities at 30 June 2026 ([Tether](https://tether.io/news/tether-posts-strong-q2-performance-generates-1-5b-net-operating-profit-maintains-4-11b-reserve-buffer-and-expands-gold-holdings-to-more-than-146-tons/)), and Circle reported $73.16bn segregated for holders at the same date, $61.9bn of it in the Circle Reserve Fund ([Circle 10-Q](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0001876042/000187604226000248/crcl-20260630.htm)). Chainalysis measured cross-border stablecoin transfers of $220.3bn in the year to June 2026, up 77.5% ([Chainalysis](https://www.chainalysis.com/blog/2026-global-crypto-adoption-index/)).

Regulation outside the UK is at two different stages. The EU's rules for e-money tokens have applied since 30 June 2024 and give holders a right to redeem at face value ([EUR-Lex](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/EN/legal-content/summary/european-crypto-assets-regulation-mica.html); [European supervisors](https://www.esma.europa.eu/sites/default/files/2025-10/Joint_ESAs_Factsheet_on_crypto-assets.pdf)). The US GENIUS Act was enacted on 18 July 2025, bars issuers from paying interest, and states that payment stablecoins "shall not be backed by the full faith and credit of the United States" ([GENIUS Act](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/PLAW-119publ27/html/PLAW-119publ27.htm)); it takes effect on the earlier of 18 January 2027 or 120 days after final rules, and the rules were still proposals in mid-July 2026 ([Chapman and Cutler](https://www.chapman.com/assets/htmldocuments/Chapman-GENIUS-Act-Rulemaking-and-Reporting-Tracker-071626.pdf)). The UK position is in the UK section below.

### Bitcoin is held rather than spent, and tracked institutions hold about a fifth

Bitcoin's supply schedule is the most teachable fact in crypto because it can be read from the chain itself. The subsidy began at 50 BTC, halves every 210,000 blocks, and has been 3.125 BTC since block 840,000; the ceiling is **20,999,999.9769 BTC**, not a round 21 million ([Bitcoin Wiki](https://en.bitcoin.it/wiki/Controlled_supply)). Mining is repeated guessing, not puzzle-solving: hardware "iterates through every possible value for the block header nonce" until a hash falls below a target ([Bitcoin developer guide](https://developer.bitcoin.org/devguide/mining.html)), and the network retunes that target every 2,016 blocks, so more machines mean more electricity, not more bitcoin ([Bitcoin developer guide](https://developer.bitcoin.org/devguide/block_chain.html)). Ether is the useful contrast: no supply cap, secured by staking since 15 September 2022, with part of every fee destroyed ([ethereum.org](https://ethereum.org/en/eth/); [ethereum.org](https://ethereum.org/en/roadmap/merge/)).

Institutional access is real and specific. The SEC approved spot bitcoin exchange-traded products on 10 January 2024 while stating "we did not approve or endorse bitcoin" ([SEC](https://www.sec.gov/newsroom/speeches-statements/gensler-statement-spot-bitcoin-011023)). The US Strategic Bitcoin Reserve created in March 2025 is funded with forfeited coins and permits only budget-neutral acquisition ([White House](https://www.whitehouse.gov/presidential-actions/2025/03/establishment-of-the-strategic-bitcoin-reserve-and-united-states-digital-asset-stockpile/)). A UK adult can buy coins directly or, since 8 October 2025, buy a crypto exchange-traded note on a UK recognised exchange, with no FSCS cover either way ([FCA](https://www.fca.org.uk/news/press-releases/fca-opens-retail-access-crypto-etns)). Those notes moved from the stocks and shares ISA to the Innovative Finance ISA on 6 April 2026 and can sit in a registered pension ([HMRC](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/tax-treatment-of-cryptoasset-exchange-traded-notes/tax-treatment-of-cryptoasset-exchange-traded-notes-policy)).

The "store of value" story is safe to teach only as contested. Bitcoin fell "in lockstep with the S&P 500" in the March 2020 crash ([Conlon and McGee](https://ideas.repec.org/a/eee/finlet/v35y2020ics1544612320304244.html)), and one peer-reviewed study finds it rises after inflation shocks but, unlike gold, falls on financial-uncertainty shocks ([Choi and Shin](https://ideas.repec.org/a/eee/finlet/v46y2022ipbs1544612321003810.html)). The evidence is mostly from 2020 to 2022, so it shows the claim was unsupported then, not what correlations are now.

The dated figures: on 4 October 2026 the chain stood at block 969,871 with about **20.09 million BTC issued, 95.68% of the cap** (computed from the schedule), and the next halving falls at block 1,050,000, around April 2028 ([mempool.space](https://mempool.space/api/blocks/tip/height); [Bitcoin Wiki](https://en.bitcoin.it/wiki/Controlled_supply)). Bitcoin's price was $85,229 that day, 32% below its record of $126,080 on 6 October 2025 ([CoinGecko](https://api.coingecko.com/api/v3/coins/bitcoin)), after trading near $58,500 on 1 July 2026 ([mempool.space](https://mempool.space/api/v1/historical-price?currency=USD&timestamp=1782864000)). The latest complete holder breakdown is from 30 November 2025: funds, companies, governments and other tracked entities held just over 4 million BTC, **about 19% of the cap**, with funds near 1.5 million and public companies 1.06 million ([BitcoinTreasuries.net](https://blog.bitcointreasuries.net/content/files/2025/12/FINAL-_-November-Adoption-Report.pdf)). One company, Strategy, held 847,363 BTC in mid-June 2026 ([BitcoinTreasuries.net](https://assets-prod.bitcointreasuries.net/files/019f3e6a-8302-7000-b3f9-619db5e9668e.pdf)). On energy, Cambridge put mining at 138 TWh a year on mid-2024 data with 52.4% from sustainable sources including nuclear, drawn from firms representing 48% of mining activity ([Cambridge](https://jbs.cam.ac.uk/2025/cambridge-study-sustainable-energy-rising-in-bitcoin-mining/)); Digiconomist displayed 204 TWh on 4 October 2026 using a different method ([Digiconomist](https://digiconomist.net/bitcoin-energy-consumption)).

As a payment tool bitcoin is small. The Federal Reserve's household survey found 2% of US adults used crypto to pay for something in 2024 against 7% who held it as an investment ([Federal Reserve](https://www.federalreserve.gov/publications/2025-economic-well-being-of-us-households-in-2024-banking-and-credit.htm)). The main community map listed 27,182 merchants worldwide on 4 October 2026 and **363 in the UK** on 26 September 2026, with 53% and 36% of listings respectively marked as recently verified ([BTC Map](https://btcmap.org)). Public Lightning Network capacity was about 3,794 BTC on 30 August 2026 ([mempool.space](https://mempool.space/api/v1/lightning/statistics/latest)). Spending bitcoin in the UK is also a Capital Gains Tax disposal ([HMRC](https://www.gov.uk/hmrc-internal-manuals/cryptoassets-manual/crypto22100)).

### Binance leads, derivatives dominate, and decentralised exchanges carry about a fifth of spot

A centralised exchange is a company that matches orders in price-then-time priority, at the price of the order already resting on its book ([Coinbase](https://docs.cdp.coinbase.com/exchange/concepts/matching-engine)), and that holds customers' coins in its own wallets. The custodian is the risk: the FBI attributes the theft of about **$1.5bn from Bybit** on or about 21 February 2025 to North Korea ([FBI](https://www.ic3.gov/PSA/2025/PSA250226)). A decentralised exchange is non-custodial software: in the classic design traders swap against a pool of two tokens whose price is the ratio of the reserves, and every trade moves that price ([Uniswap v2 whitepaper](https://app.uniswap.org/whitepaper.pdf)). Because pending transactions are not processed in a single queue, block builders can reorder them, which is what makes "sandwich" attacks on swaps possible ([ESMA](https://www.esma.europa.eu/sites/default/files/2025-07/ESMA50-481369926-29744_Maximal_Extractable_Value_Implications_for_crypto_markets.pdf)).

Most trading is not spot at all. Perpetual futures give "leveraged exposure to their underlying without rollover or direct ownership" and stay near the spot price because longs and shorts pay each other a funding rate ([He, Manela, Ross and von Wachter](https://arxiv.org/abs/2212.06888)); positions are closed by forced liquidation when equity falls below the maintenance margin ([Hyperliquid](https://hyperliquid.gitbook.io/hyperliquid-docs/trading/liquidations)). For a UK audience the safe facts are regulatory. The FCA has barred firms from selling crypto derivatives to retail clients since 6 January 2021 and warned that any firm offering them to retail consumers "is likely to be a scam" ([FCA](https://www.fca.org.uk/news/press-releases/fca-bans-sale-crypto-derivatives-retail-consumers)); in August 2025 it confirmed the ban "will remain in place" ([FCA](https://www.fca.org.uk/news/press-releases/fca-opens-retail-access-crypto-etns)), and the Handbook rule was read in force on 4 October 2026 ([FCA Handbook](https://www.handbook.fca.org.uk/handbook/COBS/22/6.html)). The BIS adds a sobering spot-market fact: over August 2015 to December 2022 "a majority of crypto app users in nearly all economies made losses on their bitcoin holdings" ([BIS Bulletin 69](https://www.bis.org/publ/bisbull69.htm)).

The dated figures: in August 2026 centralised spot volume was $891bn, Binance held 26.5% of it, and derivatives were $3.40tn of a $4.29tn total; decentralised exchanges handled $205bn of spot, **an 18.7% share**, and $549bn of futures ([CoinDesk Data](https://www.coindesk.com/research/exchange-review-august-2026)). USDT and USDC accounted for 97.7% of stablecoin trading pairs on the top exchanges ([CoinGecko](https://www.coingecko.com/research/publications/spot-centralized-exchanges-report-2026)). On 10 October 2025 more than $19bn of leveraged positions were liquidated in about a day while order-book depth on key venues shrank by more than 90% ([FTI Consulting](https://www.fticonsulting.com/insights/articles/crypto-crash-october-2025-leverage-met-liquidity)). Over the 30 days to 4 October 2026 Uniswap carried about a third of decentralised-exchange volume and Solana carried more than Ethereum mainnet, 26.8% against 14.8% (computed from [DefiLlama](https://api.llama.fi/overview/dexs)). Measured sandwich losses on Ethereum were about $60m in the year to October 2025 and falling ([Cointelegraph Research](https://cointelegraph.com/research/exclusive-data-from-eigenphi-reveals-that-sandwich-attacks-on-ethereum-have-waned)).

### Lending, staking and tokenised assets are live, measurable and narrower than their headlines

On-chain lending is pooled, over-collateralised and enforced by code. Suppliers deposit into a pool, borrowers lock collateral worth more than they borrow, rates rise with utilisation, and "a liquidation in the Aave Protocol occurs when a borrower's health factor falls below 1" ([Aave](https://aave.com/docs/aave-v3/overview); [Aave](https://aave.com/help/borrowing/liquidations)). Over-collateralisation exists because borrowers are anonymous ([BIS Bulletin 57](https://www.bis.org/publ/bisbull57.htm)). Aave, the largest protocol, held **$19.34bn net of borrowing with $13.18bn lent out** on 4 October 2026, down from a peak of $45.81bn on 7 October 2025 (computed from [DefiLlama](https://api.llama.fi/protocol/aave)); all DeFi protocols together held $95.8bn ([DefiLlama](https://api.llama.fi/v2/historicalChainTvl)). The April 2026 Kelp incident is the cleanest teaching case in the research: a bridge with a single verifier accepted a forged message, 116,500 rsETH was released, the attacker used it as collateral to borrow about $190.9m of WETH on Aave, and although "Aave's smart contracts were not compromised at any point", several WETH pools hit 100% utilisation so suppliers could not withdraw, with modelled bad debt of $123.7m to $230.1m ([Aave governance](https://governance.aave.com/t/rseth-incident-report-april-20-2026/24580)).

Staking secures Ethereum. Validators deposit at least 32 ETH, one is picked to propose a block every 12 seconds, and provable dishonesty is punished by slashing ([ethereum.org](https://ethereum.org/en/developers/docs/consensus-mechanisms/pos/)). On 4 October 2026 two trackers gave near-identical readings: 43.7 million ETH staked (35.78% of supply) at 2.63% a year ([validatorqueue.com](https://www.validatorqueue.com/)) and 43.9 million at 2.5% ([ethereum.org](https://ethereum.org/en/staking/)). Joining took about 26 days and leaving about 14 ([validatorqueue.com](https://www.validatorqueue.com/)). Lido, a pooled "liquid staking" protocol that keeps 10% of rewards ([Lido](https://docs.lido.fi/)), accounted for about 22.5% of staked ETH (computed from the [stETH contract](https://etherscan.io/token/0xae7ab96520DE3A18E5e111B5EaAb095312D7fE84)).

Tokenised real-world assets are small and mostly institutional. rwa.xyz showed **$38.73bn** of transferable tokenised assets excluding stablecoins on 4 October 2026, of which $14.82bn was tokenised US Treasury products ([rwa.xyz](https://app.rwa.xyz/); [rwa.xyz](https://app.rwa.xyz/treasuries)). International securities regulators call tokenisation "growing but ... nascent" ([IOSCO, reproduced by Mondo Visione](https://mondovisione.com/media-and-resources/news/iosco-publishes-final-report-on-financial-asset-tokenization-20251111/)), and the BIS describes tokenised money market funds as securities that circulate on public blockchains while carrying the liquidity risks of ordinary money market funds ([BIS Bulletin 115](https://www.bis.org/publ/bisbull115.htm)). The UK is moving from pilot to first permissions: the Digital Securities Sandbox had 16 entrants on 21 September 2026, with HSBC and ClearToken approved to go live ([Bank of England](https://www.bankofengland.co.uk/financial-stability/digital-securities-sandbox/digital-securities-sandbox-dashboard)); the first digital gilt is planned "by Q1 2027" and has not been issued ([HM Treasury](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/update-on-the-digital-gilt-instrument-digit-pilot-issuance/update-on-the-digital-gilt-instrument-digit-pilot-issuance)); and seven banks completed a tokenised sterling deposit pilot in September 2026 ([UK Finance](https://www.ukfinance.org.uk/tokenised-sterling-deposits)).

Where money is lost is itself a market fact. Chainalysis counted $2.2bn stolen in 2024, with private-key compromises 43.8% of it, and over $3.4bn in 2025 ([Chainalysis](https://www.chainalysis.com/blog/crypto-hacking-stolen-funds-2025/); [Chainalysis](https://www.chainalysis.com/blog/crypto-hacking-stolen-funds-2026/)). DefiLlama's incident database records $2.24bn across 286 incidents in 2026 to 1 October, led by key compromise ($704m) and bridge failures ($700m) (computed from [DefiLlama](https://api.llama.fi/hacks)). The two sources never agree on a year's total because they count different things, so every theft figure needs its source named.

## Mechanics: primary documentation supports every step, but "immutable" and "not your keys" overstate it

The mechanics notes are the strongest in the set because almost everything was read in protocol documentation: the Bitcoin developer reference and guide, the whitepaper, ethereum.org, and the BIPs and EIPs themselves. That gives lessons 1 to 3 a sourced sentence for nearly every animation step. The recurring finding is that the standard beginner corrections ("blockchains are immutable", "not your keys, not your coins", "lost keys can never be recovered") are themselves too absolute, and the accurate versions are conditional.

### What a lesson can state flatly

A Bitcoin block header is **80 bytes with six fields**, two of which do the work a beginner needs to see: the hash of the previous block's header and a Merkle root summarising the block's transactions ([Bitcoin developer reference](https://developer.bitcoin.org/reference/block_chain.html)). Change one old transaction and its ID changes, the Merkle root changes, the header hash changes and no longer meets the proof-of-work target, and the next block's stored link no longer matches, so a transaction "cannot be modified without modifying the block that records it and all following blocks" ([Bitcoin developer guide](https://developer.bitcoin.org/devguide/block_chain.html)). NIST supplies the careful wording: it is "computationally infeasible" to find an input for a given hash, not impossible ([NIST](https://csrc.nist.gov/glossary/term/cryptographic_hash_function)). Other nodes keep their own copies and "accept the block only if all transactions in it are valid and not already spent" ([Bitcoin whitepaper](https://nakamotoinstitute.org/library/bitcoin/)). Consensus differs by chain: Bitcoin settlement is gradual, with six confirmations the long-standing recommendation for high-value transfers ([bitcoin.org](https://bitcoin.org/en/you-need-to-know)); Ethereum proposes a block every 12-second slot, groups 32 slots into an epoch, and finalises in about 15 minutes, after which reverting a block would cost an attacker at least a third of all staked ETH ([ethereum.org](https://ethereum.org/en/developers/docs/consensus-mechanisms/pos/); [ethereum.org](https://ethereum.org/en/roadmap/single-slot-finality/)).

History has been rewritten, which is why "immutable" is the wrong word. Bitcoin's own chain was displaced after a bug created 184 billion bitcoins in block 74638 in August 2010 ([Bitcoin Wiki](https://en.bitcoin.it/wiki/Value_overflow_incident)) and split in March 2013 ([BIP-50](https://github.com/bitcoin/bips/blob/master/bip-0050.mediawiki)); MIT recorded "over 40 reorgs" six or more blocks deep on smaller proof-of-work coins in 2019 and 2020 ([MIT DCI](http://www.dci.mit.edu/projects/51-percent-attacks)); Ethereum Classic lost **807,260 ETC** to a double-spend in 2020 ([Bitquery](https://bitquery.io/blog/attacker-stole-807k-etc-in-ethereum-classic-51-attack)); and Monero suffered an 18-block reorganisation in September 2025 ([Decrypt](https://decrypt.co/339424/moneros-largest-reorg-erases-36-minutes-transaction-history)). The researchers found no documented successful 51% double-spend against Bitcoin or post-Merge Ethereum, so the safe line is "has not happened on Bitcoin or Ethereum; has happened repeatedly on smaller chains", and the whitepaper limits what such an attacker could do: reverse his own recent payments, not take "money that never belonged to the attacker" ([Bitcoin whitepaper](https://nakamotoinstitute.org/library/bitcoin/)).

A private key produces a public key, which is hashed into an address, and "you cannot derive a private key from public keys" ([ethereum.org](https://ethereum.org/en/developers/docs/accounts/)). A wallet is "an interface or application", not the account; it holds keys and shows the ledger, and on Bitcoin a balance "really means that you have 10,000 satoshis waiting in one or more UTXOs" ([ethereum.org](https://ethereum.org/en/developers/docs/accounts/); [Bitcoin developer guide](https://developer.bitcoin.org/devguide/transactions.html)). A 12-word recovery phrase encodes **128 bits of randomness plus a 4-bit checksum**, drawn from a fixed list of 2,048 words, which is why a wallet must generate the phrase rather than the user choosing words ([BIP-39](https://github.com/bitcoin/bips/blob/master/bip-0039.mediawiki)); one phrase regenerates a whole tree of keys across coins and addresses ([BIP-32](https://github.com/bitcoin/bips/blob/master/bip-0032.mediawiki); [BIP-44](https://github.com/bitcoin/bips/blob/master/bip-0044.mediawiki)). In self-custody the phrase "is the only way you'll be able to recover your wallet", and with an exchange account "you're trusting that exchange with custody over your funds" ([ethereum.org](https://ethereum.org/en/wallets/)). Hardware wallets keep keys on a device that runs nothing else, and funds lost from a self-custodied wallet are gone permanently ([bitcoin.org](https://bitcoin.org/en/secure-your-wallet); [bitcoin.org](https://bitcoin.org/en/you-need-to-know)). Two 2025 changes keep a wallet lesson current: since the Pectra upgrade on 7 May 2025 an ordinary Ethereum account can delegate to smart-contract code, enabling recovery schemes and sponsored fees ([ethereum.org](https://ethereum.org/en/roadmap/pectra/); [EIP-7702](https://eips.ethereum.org/EIPS/eip-7702)), and since Fusaka on 3 December 2025 the protocol natively supports the signature type behind passkeys, which Safe's documentation describes as key pairs held in the device's secure hardware with no seed phrase to write down ([ethereum.org](https://ethereum.org/en/roadmap/fusaka/); [Safe](https://docs.safe.global/advanced/passkeys/overview)).

A Bitcoin payment spends previous outputs in full, sends change back to the sender, and leaves the difference as the fee, which rises "as demand increases" for block space ([Bitcoin developer guide](https://developer.bitcoin.org/devguide/transactions.html)). An Ethereum transfer uses **21,000 units of gas** and pays gas used multiplied by the base fee plus a tip; the base fee moves by at most 12.5% per block and "is always burned", paid to nobody ([ethereum.org](https://ethereum.org/en/developers/docs/gas/); [EIP-1559](https://eips.ethereum.org/EIPS/eip-1559)). Fees depend on transaction size or gas, never on the amount sent; a failed Ethereum transaction still pays for the work done ([ethereum.org](https://ethereum.org/en/developers/docs/gas/)); and "a Bitcoin transaction cannot be reversed, it can only be refunded by the person receiving the funds" ([bitcoin.org](https://bitcoin.org/en/you-need-to-know)). The user error worth animating is address poisoning: scammers plant a look-alike address in a victim's history by sending a tiny transfer, and one campaign using 82,031 fake addresses took **$69.7m** ([Chainalysis](https://www.chainalysis.com/blog/address-poisoning-scam/)); in December 2025 an investor who had sent a test payment to the correct address then copied a poisoned one whose "first three and last four characters matched" and lost about $50m ([ForkLog](https://forklog.com/en/news/investor-loses-nearly-50-million-in-address-spoofing-attack)).

A rollup runs transactions on its own chain and posts compressed data to Ethereum, since March 2024 mostly in "blobs" that nodes prune after about 18 days ([EIP-4844](https://eips.ethereum.org/EIPS/eip-4844)). Optimistic rollups accept batches unless challenged within "roughly seven days", so native withdrawals wait that long; ZK-rollups submit a validity proof and exit without delay ([ethereum.org](https://ethereum.org/en/developers/docs/scaling/optimistic-rollups/); [ethereum.org](https://ethereum.org/en/developers/docs/scaling/zk-rollups/)). Most run a single sequencer that "can censor users by going offline" ([ethereum.org](https://ethereum.org/en/developers/docs/scaling/optimistic-rollups/)), and L2BEAT's stage framework ranks how far users must trust the operator: only at Stage 2 is the proof system permissionless and the exit window 30 days ([L2BEAT](https://l2beat.com/stages)). Bridges hold assets on one chain and issue a representation on another; wrapped bitcoin is "not the original asset on the Bitcoin blockchain" ([ethereum.org](https://ethereum.org/en/bridges/)). They are the most exploited component: $2bn was stolen in 13 bridge hacks by August 2022 ([Chainalysis](https://www.chainalysis.com/blog/cross-chain-bridge-hacks-2022/)), and in April 2026 about $292m of rsETH was released by a forged message accepted by a single verifier although "no smart contract was broken; every contract involved operated exactly as designed" ([OpenZeppelin](https://openzeppelin.com/news/lessons-from-kelpdao-hack)).

Privacy completes the set. "Bitcoin is probably the most transparent payment network in the world"; all transactions are "public, traceable, and permanently stored", and addresses carry no name but "become tainted by the history of all transactions they are involved with" ([bitcoin.org](https://bitcoin.org/en/protect-your-privacy)). The right word is pseudonymous, and the FBI's tracing of Bybit's stolen funds "across thousands of addresses on multiple blockchains" shows what that means in practice ([FBI](https://www.ic3.gov/PSA/2025/PSA250226)).

### Figures that need a date

On 4 October 2026 there were **25,208 reachable Bitcoin nodes** ([BTC Nodes](https://btcnodes.io/)) and **12,105 discoverable Ethereum nodes**, 9.55% of them hosted in the UK ([Etherscan](https://etherscan.io/nodetracker)). The Bitcoin figure carries a provenance warning: the long-running bitnodes.io tracker now redirects to a rebuilt service under a new operator that does not explain its method ([bitnodes.io](https://bitnodes.io/)). Fees were very low that day: Bitcoin's fastest tier was 1 sat/vB with lower tiers below that ([mempool.space](https://mempool.space/api/v1/fees/precise)), and Ethereum's base fee was 0.174 gwei ([Etherscan](https://etherscan.io/gastracker)), which the researchers compute as about one US cent for a simple transfer; ethereum.org's live averages were $0.071 per transaction on Ethereum and $0.0018 on layer 2 networks ([ethereum.org](https://ethereum.org/en/layer-2/)). Those are market prices: the base fee can double in six consecutive full blocks (computed from the 12.5% rule), and no 2026 peak figure was captured. One exchange credits Bitcoin deposits after four confirmations, "approximately 40 minutes" ([Kraken](https://support.kraken.com/articles/203325283-cryptocurrency-deposit-processing-times)). On layer 2, L2BEAT rated four small projects Stage 2 and Arbitrum One, Base and OP Mainnet Stage 1 on 4 October 2026 ([L2BEAT](https://l2beat.com/scaling/summary)), with Base securing $16.25bn and Arbitrum One $11.52bn ([L2BEAT](https://l2beat.com/scaling/tvs)); layer 2 fees fell by around 95% when blobs arrived in March 2024, on growthepie data ([DL News](https://www.dlnews.com/articles/defi/ethereums-dencun-upgrade-cuts-layer-2-fees-as-much-as-98/)). Blob capacity has risen in steps to a target of 14 and maximum of 21 per block since 7 January 2026 ([Ethereum Foundation](https://blog.ethereum.org/2025/11/06/fusaka-mainnet-announcement)). The next Ethereum upgrade, Glamsterdam, had a testnet date of 6 October 2026 and no mainnet date ([Ethereum Foundation](https://blog.ethereum.org/en/2026/09/17/glamsterdam-testnet-announcement)); none of its listed changes alters slot time or finality.

### Visual metaphors that contradict the sources

Because the lessons are animated, the researchers flagged metaphors that would misstate the documented mechanism. These are inferences, not sourced claims, but each follows directly from the documentation cited above. Coins do not travel between wallets; on Bitcoin old outputs are consumed and new ones created on the ledger, and on Ethereum two balances change. A wallet is closer to a keyring than a purse, and a hardware wallet is not a memory stick holding coins. A seed phrase is a master-key backup, not a password: it cannot be reset, and anyone holding it derives every key. A hash is a fingerprint and is never "decrypted" or "unlocked". Mining is a lottery of guesses, not a puzzle being solved. A padlock clicking shut suits Ethereum's justified-then-finalised step but misdescribes Bitcoin, where confidence accumulates with each confirmation and never becomes absolute. The mempool is not one global queue but many nodes each holding pending transactions. A rollup is a separate chain that files compressed records and proofs with Ethereum, not an express lane on the same road; a bridge locks assets on one side and issues a representation on the other, which is exactly why a forged message can release tokens with nothing behind them. And a zero-knowledge proof attests that execution was correct; it does not hide the transactions.

## UK context: 8% of adults hold crypto under rules that do not fully start until 25 October 2027

The UK note sorts its material into verbatim readings (from PDFs) and extracted readings (text returned by an automated page summariser). The substance of both is usable; the exact wording of extracted items, in particular the FCA's prescribed risk warning and the HMRC guidance pages, must be checked against the live page before anything is quoted. Items the researchers marked for a lawyer or an accountant are flagged the same way here.

### Adoption: fewer, more committed holders

The FCA's sixth consumer survey, with fieldwork from 5 August to 2 September 2025 and published on 16 December 2025, found **8% of UK adults holding crypto, down from 12% in 2024** and still double the 2021 level; 91% had heard of it ([FCA Wave 6](https://www.fca.org.uk/publication/research-notes/cryptoasset-consumer-research-2025-wave-6.pdf)). Holders skew male (11% against 5%), young (15% of 18 to 34-year-olds against 2% of over-55s) and higher social grade, and their holdings are small: **64% hold £1,000 or less and 27% hold £100 or less** (summed from the survey's bands). Bitcoin is held by 57% of owners and Ethereum by 43%. Three-quarters buy through a centralised exchange. What they do with it is the single most important adoption fact for the lessons: 45% have converted to cash, 37% have swapped for other crypto, 17% have ever bought goods or services, and 34% have never used their holdings at all. A fifth (22%) have staked, 44% have not heard of staking, and 7% lent or borrowed crypto in the past year. Only half of users say they have a good understanding of how crypto works, and the top reason non-owners give for staying out is not understanding it (22%). The FCA warns that the online panel "could overestimate the number of cryptoasset users in the UK", and the 62% reporting a net gain was measured after a strong market and will move with prices ([FCA Wave 6](https://www.fca.org.uk/publication/research-notes/cryptoasset-consumer-research-2025-wave-6.pdf)). Chainalysis ranked the UK 17th in its 2026 global adoption index ([Chainalysis](https://www.chainalysis.com/blog/2026-global-crypto-adoption-index/)).

### Regulation: registered now, authorised from 25 October 2027

Today crypto is regulated in the UK in two respects. Firms have had to register with the FCA under the Money Laundering Regulations since January 2020, and that registration is for anti-money-laundering supervision only ([FCA](https://www.fca.org.uk/cryptoassets)). Since 8 October 2023 crypto promotions to UK consumers have been a regulated activity with four lawful routes, and promoting outside them breaches section 21 of FSMA, a criminal offence carrying up to two years' imprisonment ([FCA](https://www.fca.org.uk/firms/cryptoassets/marketing-uk-consumers)). Registration is also hard to get: of 386 applications from January 2020 to the end of 2025, 62 were approved and 257 withdrawn ([FCA](https://www.fca.org.uk/freedom-information/information-authorisation-crypto-firms-registrations-march-2026)). The FCA's own consumer line remains that "crypto is largely unregulated in the UK" and that consumers should "not expect any kind of compensation to cover any form of crypto-related losses" ([FCA](https://www.fca.org.uk/consumers/cryptoassets)).

The full regime is legislated and scheduled. The Financial Services and Markets Act 2000 (Cryptoassets) Regulations 2026 were made on 4 February 2026 and create new regulated activities, including issuing qualifying stablecoins, safeguarding, operating a trading platform, dealing, arranging and staking ([legislation.gov.uk](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/uksi/2026/102/contents/made)). The FCA published five final policy statements on 30 June 2026, the application window runs from **30 September 2026 to 28 February 2027**, and the regime starts on **25 October 2027**; existing registrations get "no automatic conversion" ([FCA](https://www.fca.org.uk/publications/policy-statements/cryptoasset-regime); [FCA](https://www.fca.org.uk/firms/new-regime-cryptoasset-regulation/how-gateway-will-operate)). Under those rules UK-issued stablecoins must be backed 1:1 with assets held on trust, redeemable at par within one business day, with no interest passed to holders ([FCA PS26/10](https://www.fca.org.uk/publication/policy/ps26-10.pdf)); retail lending and borrowing will be permitted with "express prior consent", an appropriateness test, over-collateralisation and negative balance protection, and the FCA stresses that crypto "lending" involves "a disposal of the assets to the recipient" ([FCA PS26/11](https://www.fca.org.uk/publication/policy/ps26-11.pdf)). Staking is lawful now, has been expressly excluded from the collective-investment-scheme rules since 31 January 2025 ([legislation.gov.uk](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/uksi/2025/17/made)), and becomes a specifically authorised activity in October 2027. Separately, the Bank of England's regime for "systemic" sterling stablecoins replaced its proposed £20,000 individual holding cap with a temporary £40bn cap on each coin's total issuance in June 2026; that Code is a draft to be finalised by the end of 2026, and no coin has been recognised as systemic ([Bank of England](https://www.bankofengland.co.uk/paper/2026/ps/sterling-denominated-systemic-stablecoin); [FCA PS26/10](https://www.fca.org.uk/publication/policy/ps26-10.pdf)). Two other 2025 proposals did not survive: the FCA's January 2026 consultation said "we do not propose restricting firms from accepting credit card payments" for crypto ([FCA CP26/4](https://www.fca.org.uk/publication/consultation/cp26-4.pdf)), and the retail derivatives ban stayed while retail access to exchange-traded notes opened on 8 October 2025 ([FCA](https://www.fca.org.uk/news/press-releases/fca-opens-retail-access-crypto-etns)).

### Protection: three words beginners blur

The clean framing the research supports is that **registered**, **authorised** and **compensated** are three different things. Registered means anti-money-laundering checks, and the FCA warns that "just because a firm is registered, doesn't mean you'll have access to the FSCS or the Financial Ombudsman if something goes wrong" ([FCA](https://www.fca.org.uk/consumers/crypto-investment-scams)). Authorised, from 25 October 2027, brings conduct rules and a complaints route: the Ombudsman "will only be able to consider complaints against qualifying cryptoasset firms arising from acts or omissions ... on or after the day the new regime goes live", and "it is unlikely that redress will be owed" for losses caused by market falls ([FCA CP26/4](https://www.fca.org.uk/publication/consultation/cp26-4.pdf); [FCA PS26/13](https://www.fca.org.uk/publication/policy/ps26-13.pdf)). Compensated is what crypto does not get: "We do not plan to extend FSCS cover to new regulated cryptoasset activities" ([FCA PS26/13](https://www.fca.org.uk/publication/policy/ps26-13.pdf)), and the same applies to retail crypto exchange-traded notes ([FCA](https://www.fca.org.uk/news/press-releases/fca-opens-retail-access-crypto-etns)). The gap in public understanding is measurable: 13% of UK crypto users believe they would have financial protection, rising to 20% of users aged 18 to 34 ([FCA Wave 6](https://www.fca.org.uk/publication/research-notes/cryptoasset-consumer-research-2025-wave-6.pdf)). The bank-transfer reimbursement scheme that has refunded authorised push payment fraud up to £85,000 since 7 October 2024 covers UK bank-to-bank payments and is not a crypto protection ([Payment Systems Regulator](https://www.psr.org.uk/information-for-consumers/app-fraud-reimbursement-protections/)).

### The line the site itself must not cross

EndorseCrypto is neither authorised nor registered, so it has no lawful route to make a crypto promotion; its content must stay on the "inform and educate" side of section 21. The FCA's guidance sets the test. The restriction catches anyone who, "in the course of business", communicates "an invitation or inducement to engage in investment activity", and the business test "is intended to capture any level of commerciality", including anticipated revenue; payment is not required, audience size is irrelevant, and memes count ([FCA FG24/1](https://www.fca.org.uk/publication/finalised-guidance/fg24-1.pdf)). The perimeter guidance says communications "of purely factual information" fall outside, provided the facts are presented so that they do not amount to an invitation or inducement, and "links which are activated merely by clicking on a name or logo will not be inducements" ([FCA Handbook PERG 8.4](https://handbook.fca.org.uk/handbook/PERG/8/4.html)). Affiliate links carry a second risk: earning commission by introducing customers to an investment firm "may need to consider whether that activity involves them carrying on regulated arranging activity", itself a criminal offence without authorisation ([FCA FG24/1](https://www.fca.org.uk/publication/finalised-guidance/fg24-1.pdf)). Enforcement is live; in April 2026 the FCA secured a guilty plea from a reality-television personality for illegal promotions and made 120 takedown requests covering 1,267 adverts ([FCA](https://www.fca.org.uk/news/press-releases/fca-spearheads-global-action-stop-illegal-finfluencers)). The researchers' inferences, all marked for a lawyer, are that a limited company's website is almost certainly "in the course of business"; that naming a platform with a call to action, price predictions, "best exchange" rankings, referral links and urgency push content toward inducement; that a "not financial advice" label changes nothing because the test is objective; and that the brand name "Endorse" could itself read as a signal of recommendation. The company must also show its registered name, number, registered office and place of registration on the site ([legislation.gov.uk](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/uksi/2015/17/part/6)) and, once it holds personal data, pay the ICO fee, £52 for a micro organisation on 4 October 2026 ([ICO](https://ico.org.uk/for-organisations/data-protection-fee/data-protection-fee)).

### Tax: there is no crypto tax, only disposals and income

HMRC treats a disposal as selling, "exchanging them for a different type of cryptoasset", "using them to pay for goods or services" or giving them away, with gains pooled share-style per token type and matched same-day first, then within 30 days, then against the pool ([gov.uk](https://www.gov.uk/guidance/check-if-you-need-to-pay-tax-when-you-sell-cryptoassets); [HMRC](https://www.gov.uk/hmrc-internal-manuals/cryptoassets-manual/crypto22200)). Tokens received "from employment, or from activities such as mining, staking or lending count as income", and a later sale is a separate capital gains event ([gov.uk](https://www.gov.uk/guidance/check-if-you-need-to-pay-tax-when-you-receive-cryptoassets)). Staking rewards for a non-trader are "taxable as income (miscellaneous income)" at their sterling value when received ([HMRC](https://www.gov.uk/hmrc-internal-manuals/cryptoassets-manual/crypto21200)); depositing tokens into a DeFi lending or liquidity arrangement can itself be a disposal where beneficial ownership passes ([HMRC](https://www.gov.uk/hmrc-internal-manuals/cryptoassets-manual/crypto61620)); and an airdrop may escape income tax but still produce a gain on disposal ([HMRC](https://www.gov.uk/hmrc-internal-manuals/cryptoassets-manual/crypto21250)). The dated numbers for 2026/27 are a **£3,000 annual exempt amount and rates of 18% and 24%** ([gov.uk](https://www.gov.uk/capital-gains-tax/rates)), a £1,000 trading and miscellaneous income allowance ([gov.uk](https://www.gov.uk/guidance/check-if-you-need-to-pay-tax-when-you-receive-cryptoassets)), and the Cryptoasset Reporting Framework, under which UK platforms have collected users' names, addresses, tax residence and National Insurance numbers since 1 January 2026 and will report 2026 activity to HMRC by 31 May 2027 ([gov.uk](https://www.gov.uk/guidance/reporting-cryptoasset-user-and-transaction-data)). HMRC's own line is that "this isn't a new tax" ([HMRC](https://hm-revenue-customs-hmrc.mynewsdesk.com/pressreleases/crypto-bros-being-forced-to-pay-fair-share-of-tax-3395840)). The reform most likely to be misreported is the draft published on 13 July 2026 that would put entering and leaving qualifying lending, borrowing and liquidity-pool arrangements on a "no gain, no loss" basis from 6 April 2027; it is draft legislation, it does not make returns tax-free, and it does not mention validator rewards ([gov.uk](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/cryptoasset-loans-and-liquidity-pools/tax-treatment-of-cryptoasset-loans-and-liquidity-pools)).

### Scams: seven sourced mechanics and two sets of national figures

The scam research is unusually complete because each scam type is sourced step by step. Fake investment platforms advertise on social media "often using images of celebrities", show fabricated dashboards, and then close the account or keep extracting ([FCA](https://www.fca.org.uk/consumers/crypto-investment-scams)). Romance or "pig butchering" scams begin with a stranger's message, build a relationship, steer to a fake trading platform, allow small early withdrawals "to build false confidence", then demand "additional taxes and fees" before any withdrawal ([FINRA](https://www.finra.org/investors/insights/pig-butchering-scams)). Fake support asks victims to share screens through genuine remote-access software, which the FCA says never to do "even with companies you've contacted yourself" ([FCA](https://www.fca.org.uk/consumers/screen-sharing-scams)), or to type a recovery phrase into a fake app, which the wallet maker says it will never request ([Ledger](https://www.ledger.com/phishing-campaigns-status)). Wallet drainers work differently: the victim signs a permission, not a payment, and hands over no secret; losses on Ethereum-compatible chains were $83.85m from 106,106 victims in 2025, down 83% ([Scam Sniffer](https://drops.scamsniffer.io/scam-sniffer-2025-crypto-phishing-losses-fall-83-to-84-million/)). Address poisoning, recovery scams that charge upfront "tax, solicitor or administrative fees" to recover earlier losses ([FCA](https://www.fca.org.uk/consumers/recovery-room-scams)), and giveaways that "multiply any cryptocurrency you send them" ([FTC](https://consumer.ftc.gov/articles/what-know-about-cryptocurrency-and-scams)) complete the set. In the UK 25% of crypto users have met or suspected a scam and 7% say they were victims; the commonest types encountered were fake endorsements or giveaways (41%) and fake websites or phishing (40%) ([FCA Wave 6](https://www.fca.org.uk/publication/research-notes/cryptoasset-consumer-research-2025-wave-6.pdf)). The checks are specific: the Financial Services Register and Firm Checker, the Warning List with its caveat that a firm not on it "may still be unauthorised or be a scam", and reporting to the FCA on 0800 111 6768 and to Report Fraud, which replaced Action Fraud in December 2025 ([FCA](https://www.fca.org.uk/consumers/warning-list-unauthorised-firms); [FCA](https://www.fca.org.uk/consumers/crypto-investment-scams)). On scale, UK Finance reports criminals "stole £1.28 billion through payment fraud in 2025" ([UK Finance](https://www.ukfinance.org.uk/policy-and-guidance/reports-and-publications/annual-fraud-report-2026)), and globally Chainalysis identified "at least $154 billion" received by illicit addresses in 2025, "below 1%" of attributed volume and a lower bound it revises upward each year ([Chainalysis](https://www.chainalysis.com/blog/2026-crypto-crime-report-introduction/)), with TRM Labs at $158bn ([TRM Labs](https://www.trmlabs.com/resources/blog/2026-crypto-crime-report-key-insights-trm-identifies-record-usd-158-billion-in-illicit-crypto-flows-in-2025-reversing-a-multi-year-decline)).

## Ten lessons, each resting on sources that were opened and read

Every one of the ten lessons has a primary-sourced mechanism at its core. The pattern across all ten is the same: teach the mechanism from the documentation, put every market figure in a dated layer that can be replaced without rewriting the lesson, and keep the hold items out until checked. No lesson is dedicated to layer 2 or to derivatives; the research places layer 2 inside lesson 3 and derivatives inside lesson 6, where the UK retail ban makes them a matter of consumer protection rather than technique.

### Lesson 1: What is a blockchain

The lesson rests on the Bitcoin developer reference for the 80-byte header with its previous-header hash and Merkle root ([Bitcoin developer reference](https://developer.bitcoin.org/reference/block_chain.html)), the developer guide for the consequence that a transaction "cannot be modified without modifying the block that records it and all following blocks" ([Bitcoin developer guide](https://developer.bitcoin.org/devguide/block_chain.html)), NIST's definition of a hash as one-way and collision-resistant in the "computationally infeasible" sense ([NIST](https://csrc.nist.gov/glossary/term/cryptographic_hash_function)), and the whitepaper's rule that nodes accept a block only if its transactions are valid and unspent ([Bitcoin whitepaper](https://nakamotoinstitute.org/library/bitcoin/)). The planned hands-on step, changing a record and watching the link snap, matches the documented sequence exactly: transaction ID, Merkle root, header hash, then the next block's link. The pull-back to "many computers holding the same chain" uses the dated node counts, about 25,200 reachable Bitcoin nodes and 12,105 Ethereum nodes on 4 October 2026 ([BTC Nodes](https://btcnodes.io/); [Etherscan](https://etherscan.io/nodetracker)), with the Bitcoin tracker's change of operator noted. The lesson should introduce the two consensus models briefly, since lessons 4 and 8 build on them: proof of work on Bitcoin, proof of stake on Ethereum since 15 September 2022 ([ethereum.org](https://ethereum.org/en/history/)). Say "computationally infeasible", not "impossible", and show the exceptions: smaller chains reorganised ([MIT DCI](http://www.dci.mit.edu/projects/51-percent-attacks)) and Bitcoin's own 2010 fix ([Bitcoin Wiki](https://en.bitcoin.it/wiki/Value_overflow_incident)). Hold the May 2023 Ethereum finality pause.

### Lesson 2: Keys and wallets

The key-to-address chain is sourced on both networks: random private key, derived public key, hashed address, no way back ([Bitcoin developer guide](https://developer.bitcoin.org/devguide/transactions.html); [ethereum.org](https://ethereum.org/en/developers/docs/accounts/)). The twelve recovery words dealt out "like cards" are 128 bits of entropy plus a four-bit checksum mapped onto a 2,048-word list, so the wallet must generate them ([BIP-39](https://github.com/bitcoin/bips/blob/master/bip-0039.mediawiki)), and one phrase backs up every address because all keys sit on one derivation tree ([BIP-32](https://github.com/bitcoin/bips/blob/master/bip-0032.mediawiki)). The planned step where only the key can release coins sent to the address is the signature mechanism in the developer guide. The lesson must carry three qualifications the sources insist on: a wallet is "an interface", the coins are ledger entries ([ethereum.org](https://ethereum.org/en/wallets/)); an exchange account means "you're trusting that exchange with custody over your funds" ([ethereum.org](https://ethereum.org/en/wallets/)), and three-quarters of UK buyers use one ([FCA Wave 6](https://www.fca.org.uk/publication/research-notes/cryptoasset-consumer-research-2025-wave-6.pdf)); and self-custody loss is permanent unless a smart-contract or custodial recovery route existed beforehand ([bitcoin.org](https://bitcoin.org/en/you-need-to-know); [ethereum.org](https://ethereum.org/en/roadmap/account-abstraction/)). Passkey wallets deserve a sentence as a 2026 reality ([Safe](https://docs.safe.global/advanced/passkeys/overview)). The natural scam tie-in is that fake support asks for the recovery phrase and the maker never will ([Ledger](https://www.ledger.com/phishing-campaigns-status)). Hold bech32 address formats, passkey recovery caveats, adoption counts for smart accounts, and any figure for lost bitcoin.

### Lesson 3: Sending a payment

A Bitcoin payment is documented input by input: the wallet selects outputs it controls, creates a recipient output and a change output, leaves the difference as the fee, signs without ever revealing the key, and broadcasts; miners include it and each later block adds a confirmation ([Bitcoin developer guide](https://developer.bitcoin.org/devguide/transactions.html); [Bitcoin whitepaper](https://nakamotoinstitute.org/library/bitcoin/); [bitcoin.org](https://bitcoin.org/en/you-need-to-know)). The planned fee slider is sound: fees are "calculated based on current demand for space in mined blocks" and depend on transaction size, not value ([Bitcoin developer guide](https://developer.bitcoin.org/devguide/transactions.html); [BIP-141](https://github.com/bitcoin/bips/blob/master/bip-0141.mediawiki)). The Ethereum version adds gas, a burned base fee that moves at most 12.5% per block, and a tip ([ethereum.org](https://ethereum.org/en/developers/docs/gas/); [EIP-1559](https://eips.ethereum.org/EIPS/eip-1559)). Irreversibility is quotable: a transaction "cannot be reversed, it can only be refunded by the person receiving the funds" ([bitcoin.org](https://bitcoin.org/en/you-need-to-know)). This is also where layer 2 belongs: most tracked activity now runs on rollups that post data to Ethereum, fees there averaged $0.0018 against $0.071 on Ethereum on 4 October 2026 ([ethereum.org](https://ethereum.org/en/layer-2/)), optimistic withdrawals wait about seven days ([ethereum.org](https://ethereum.org/en/developers/docs/scaling/optimistic-rollups/)), and safety varies by L2BEAT stage ([L2BEAT](https://l2beat.com/stages)). Address poisoning is the sourced cautionary tale ([Chainalysis](https://www.chainalysis.com/blog/address-poisoning-scam/)). Animate ledger entries changing, not a coin flying between devices. Hold replace-by-fee, wrong-network recovery, and any "typical fee in pounds".

### Lesson 4: Bitcoin and its fixed supply

The schedule is read from the chain: 50 BTC per block at the genesis block on 3 January 2009, halving at blocks 210,000, 420,000, 630,000 and 840,000 (20 April 2024, 00:09 UTC) to 3.125 BTC today, with a ceiling of 20,999,999.9769 BTC ([Bitcoin Wiki](https://en.bitcoin.it/wiki/Controlled_supply); [mempool.space](https://mempool.space/block/0000000000000000000320283a032748cef8227873ff4872689bf23f1cda83a5)). The trigger is block height, not a date, and about 20.09 million BTC (95.68%) had been issued by 4 October 2026, with the next halving at block 1,050,000 around April 2028 ([mempool.space](https://mempool.space/api/blocks/tip/height)). Mining is guessing against a target that the network resets every 2,016 blocks, which is why more miners do not mean more bitcoin ([Bitcoin developer guide](https://developer.bitcoin.org/devguide/mining.html); [Bitcoin developer guide](https://developer.bitcoin.org/devguide/block_chain.html)). The whitepaper itself never states 21 million ([Bitcoin whitepaper](https://nakamotoinstitute.org/library/bitcoin/)). The lesson should set scarcity against volatility and holding: past falls of 75% to 93% from peaks ([CryptoSlate](https://cryptoslate.com/analysis-of-previous-bitcoin-bottoms-suggests-more-pain-to-come/)), a 2025 record of $126,080 followed by a fall of about half by July 2026 ([CoinGecko](https://api.coingecko.com/api/v3/coins/bitcoin)), no safe-haven behaviour in March 2020 ([Conlon and McGee](https://ideas.repec.org/a/eee/finlet/v35y2020ics1544612320304244.html)), tracked institutions at about 19% of the cap ([BitcoinTreasuries.net](https://blog.bitcointreasuries.net/content/files/2025/12/FINAL-_-November-Adoption-Report.pdf)), and the UK routes (direct purchase or an exchange-traded note, neither FSCS-covered) ([FCA](https://www.fca.org.uk/news/press-releases/fca-opens-retail-access-crypto-etns)). Ether is the contrast with no cap ([ethereum.org](https://ethereum.org/en/eth/)). Use "just under 21 million" and "roughly every four years". Hold the lost-coin number, the 2140 date as anything but a projection, the Cambridge December 2025 preview, ETF asset totals and government holdings.

### Lesson 5: Stablecoins

The mint-and-redeem sequence for a fiat-backed coin is sourced at every step: an approved business passes checks, wires money, receives new tokens, and later returns tokens to have them burned, with the issuer keeping the interest on reserves and bank hours constraining redemption ([Federal Reserve](https://www.federalreserve.gov/econres/notes/feds-notes/primary-and-secondary-markets-for-stablecoins-20240223.html); [Circle](https://www.circle.com/circle-mint); [Tether](https://tether.to/en/fees/)). The public buys existing tokens at a market price, which is why the three depeg stories matter: Terra ([US DOJ](https://www.justice.gov/usao-sdny/pr/crypto-enabled-fraudster-sentenced-orchestrating-40-billion-fraud); [NY Fed](https://libertystreeteconomics.newyorkfed.org/2023/07/runs-on-stablecoins/)), USDC in March 2023 ([Federal Reserve](https://www.federalreserve.gov/econres/notes/feds-notes/primary-and-secondary-markets-for-stablecoins-20240223.html)) and USDe on one exchange in October 2025 ([21Shares](https://www.21shares.com/research/why-did-ethenas-stablecoin-remain-stable-onchain-but-depegged-on-binance)). The crypto-collateralised design is sourced from the Maker whitepaper ([Maker](https://makerdao.com/en/whitepaper/)) and the regulators' definition from the European supervisors and the FCA ([ESAs](https://www.esma.europa.eu/sites/default/files/2025-10/Joint_ESAs_Factsheet_on_crypto-assets.pdf); [FCA Wave 6](https://www.fca.org.uk/publication/research-notes/cryptoasset-consumer-research-2025-wave-6.pdf)). On use, teach the Visa adjustment and the UK survey's finding that half of buyers wanted them for trading ([Visa](https://corporate.visa.com/en/sites/visa-perspectives/trends-insights/making-sense-of-stablecoins.html); [FCA Wave 6](https://www.fca.org.uk/publication/research-notes/cryptoasset-consumer-research-2025-wave-6.pdf)), and that a UK consumer can hold PYUSD in PayPal but cannot be paid in stablecoins through Stripe as a UK business ([PayPal](https://newsroom.paypal-corp.com/2026-03-17-PAYPAL-BRINGS-PAYPAL-USD-TO-USERS-ACROSS-70-MARKETS); [Stripe](https://docs.stripe.com/payments/stablecoin-payments)). The dated layer is supply ($315.76bn on DefiLlama, 4 October 2026) and the regulatory calendar (EU in force; US effective by 18 January 2027; UK rules start 25 October 2027). Every stablecoin payment is a UK tax disposal ([HMRC](https://www.gov.uk/hmrc-internal-manuals/cryptoassets-manual/crypto22100)). Hold the McKinsey payments estimate, the Visa and Mastercard settlement figures, Tether's asset-by-asset split and the smaller 2026 depegs.

### Lesson 6: Exchanges

The order-book mechanism is sourced from Coinbase's own documentation: price-time priority, execution at the resting order's price, maker and taker, three order states ([Coinbase](https://docs.cdp.coinbase.com/exchange/concepts/matching-engine)). The custody point comes from the wallet sources (the exchange holds the keys) and from the Bybit theft, attributed by the FBI to North Korea ([ethereum.org](https://ethereum.org/en/wallets/); [FBI](https://www.ic3.gov/PSA/2025/PSA250226)). The UK facts are the backbone of this lesson: 73% of UK buyers use a centralised exchange ([FCA Wave 6](https://www.fca.org.uk/publication/research-notes/cryptoasset-consumer-research-2025-wave-6.pdf)); registered is not authorised, and nothing is FSCS-compensated ([FCA](https://www.fca.org.uk/consumers/crypto-investment-scams); [FCA PS26/13](https://www.fca.org.uk/publication/policy/ps26-13.pdf)); and firms may not sell crypto derivatives to UK retail clients, so a firm offering leverage to a UK beginner "is likely to be a scam" ([FCA](https://www.fca.org.uk/news/press-releases/fca-bans-sale-crypto-derivatives-retail-consumers); [FCA Handbook](https://www.handbook.fca.org.uk/handbook/COBS/22/6.html)). The dated layer gives scale: $891bn of centralised spot volume in August 2026, Binance at 26.5%, derivatives at 79.3%, decentralised venues at 18.7% of spot ([CoinDesk Data](https://www.coindesk.com/research/exchange-review-august-2026)), and the 10 October 2025 liquidation cascade as the illustration of leverage, thin books and round-the-clock trading ([FTI Consulting](https://www.fticonsulting.com/insights/articles/crypto-crash-october-2025-leverage-met-liquidity)). A one-line contrast with a non-custodial exchange sets up lesson 7 ([Uniswap v3 whitepaper](https://app.uniswap.org/whitepaper-v3.pdf)). Hold the FTX, Mt. Gox, QuadrigaCX and Celsius case details, the proof-of-reserves warning, the wash-trading percentage, any fee percentages and the Bitget incident until the primary pages are read.

### Lesson 7: Swaps and liquidity pools

The two tanks the plan describes are the Uniswap v2 whitepaper: a pair "stores pooled reserves of two assets", the product of the reserves cannot decrease, and the price is one reserve divided by the other ([Uniswap v2 whitepaper](https://app.uniswap.org/whitepaper.pdf)). The slider can run the researchers' worked example, which follows the whitepaper's fee-then-invariant rule with made-up numbers: a pool of 10 ETH and 20,000 USDC, a 1,000 USDC purchase that pays 3 USDC in fees and receives 0.474830 ETH at an effective price of 2,106 against a starting price of 2,000, 5.03% worse, while a 100 USDC trade in the same pool is only 0.79% worse. That gives the "small pool versus large pool" comparison the plan wants, and it separates price impact from the fee and from slippage caused by others' trades. Arbitrage pulls the pool price back toward other venues ([Uniswap v2 whitepaper](https://app.uniswap.org/whitepaper.pdf)). Liquidity providers earn the fee but face impermanent loss, which the protocol's own documentation quantifies: a 2x price move costs 5.7% against holding, a 5x move 25.5% ([Uniswap](https://developers.uniswap.org/docs/protocols/v2/concepts/understanding-returns)). Sandwich attacks, slippage tolerance as the defence, and aggregators are sourced from ESMA, Uniswap and Jupiter ([ESMA](https://www.esma.europa.eu/sites/default/files/2025-07/ESMA50-481369926-29744_Maximal_Extractable_Value_Implications_for_crypto_markets.pdf); [Uniswap](https://developers.uniswap.org/docs/protocols/v2/concepts/pricing); [Jupiter](https://developers.jup.ag/docs/swap/routing)). Two UK facts must appear: a swap is a Capital Gains Tax disposal ([gov.uk](https://www.gov.uk/guidance/check-if-you-need-to-pay-tax-when-you-sell-cryptoassets)), and 37% of UK owners have made one ([FCA Wave 6](https://www.fca.org.uk/publication/research-notes/cryptoasset-consumer-research-2025-wave-6.pdf)). Hold whether providers profit today (the only study is 2021), current default slippage settings, and chain-share figures beyond "DefiLlama shows".

### Lesson 8: Staking

Every step is on ethereum.org: a 32 ETH minimum deposit and activation queue, one proposer per 12-second slot, committee attestations, checkpoints justified and finalised, rewards that fall as total stake rises, offline penalties roughly equal to missed rewards, slashing only for provable dishonesty with an initial penalty of 1/4096 of the balance, and withdrawals possible only since 12 April 2023 ([ethereum.org](https://ethereum.org/en/developers/docs/consensus-mechanisms/pos/); [ethereum.org](https://ethereum.org/en/developers/docs/consensus-mechanisms/pos/rewards-and-penalties/); [ethereum.org](https://ethereum.org/en/staking/withdrawals/)). Since 7 May 2025 a validator can hold up to 2,048 ETH ([ethereum.org](https://ethereum.org/en/roadmap/pectra/)). Liquid staking is sourced from Lido's documentation, including its 10% fee ([Lido](https://docs.lido.fi/)). The dated layer: about 43.7 to 43.9 million ETH staked (35 to 36% of supply) at 2.5 to 2.63% depending on tracker, a 26-day entry queue and 14-day exit, and Lido at about 22.5% ([validatorqueue.com](https://www.validatorqueue.com/); [ethereum.org](https://ethereum.org/en/staking/); [stETH contract](https://etherscan.io/token/0xae7ab96520DE3A18E5e111B5EaAb095312D7fE84)). The UK facts: 22% of UK users have staked and 44% have not heard of it ([FCA Wave 6](https://www.fca.org.uk/publication/research-notes/cryptoasset-consumer-research-2025-wave-6.pdf)); staking is not a collective investment scheme and becomes an authorised activity on 25 October 2027 ([legislation.gov.uk](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/uksi/2025/17/made); [FCA](https://www.fca.org.uk/publications/policy-statements/cryptoasset-regime)); and rewards are income at their sterling value on receipt ([HMRC](https://www.gov.uk/hmrc-internal-manuals/cryptoassets-manual/crypto21200)). Rewards are newly issued ETH, not interest, so their pound value moves with the price. Hold the detailed PS26/11 staking protections, the tax treatment of liquid-staking tokens, and any entity-by-entity breakdown of stake.

### Lesson 9: Lending and borrowing

Aave's documentation supplies the whole mechanism: suppliers receive interest-bearing aTokens, "supplier yields are funded by borrower interest net of the reserve factor", positions "are always over-collateralized", rates follow a two-slope utilisation curve, and the health factor formula with Aave's own example of $10,000 of ETH at an 80% threshold against a $6,000 loan giving 1.333 ([Aave](https://aave.com/docs/aave-v3/overview); [Aave](https://aave.com/help/borrowing/liquidations)). The researchers extend that example: ETH falling 25% to $1,500 brings the factor to 1, and at $1,480 a liquidator repays $3,000 of debt and takes $3,150 of collateral, so the borrower keeps the borrowed money and loses a $150 bonus plus the fall in collateral value; the 5% bonus is illustrative, since live parameters are set by governance. The BIS explains why collateral is required (anonymous borrowers) and what the borrowing is for (mostly leverage on crypto) ([BIS Bulletin 57](https://www.bis.org/publ/bisbull57.htm)). The Kelp incident teaches three separate risks, contract risk (did not fail), collateral risk (failed) and liquidity risk for suppliers (materialised) ([Aave governance](https://governance.aave.com/t/rseth-incident-report-april-20-2026/24580)). The dated layer: Aave at $19.34bn net and $13.18bn borrowed, lending protocols at $55.4bn in total, on 4 October 2026 ([DefiLlama](https://api.llama.fi/protocol/aave); [DefiLlama](https://api.llama.fi/protocols)). The UK facts: 7% of users lent or borrowed in the past year ([FCA Wave 6](https://www.fca.org.uk/publication/research-notes/cryptoasset-consumer-research-2025-wave-6.pdf)); retail lending rules with consent, appropriateness testing, over-collateralisation and negative balance protection apply from 25 October 2027, and the FCA notes crypto lending "involves a disposal of the assets" ([FCA PS26/11](https://www.fca.org.uk/publication/policy/ps26-11.pdf)); HMRC can treat the deposit as a taxable disposal under current guidance ([HMRC](https://www.gov.uk/hmrc-internal-manuals/cryptoassets-manual/crypto61620)), with draft relief from April 2027 ([gov.uk](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/cryptoasset-loans-and-liquidity-pools/tax-treatment-of-cryptoasset-loans-and-liquidity-pools)). Hold the Celsius, Voyager, BlockFi and Genesis details, the Black Thursday figures, the exact two-slope formula and the final Kelp bad-debt figure.

### Lesson 10: Tokenised assets

The mechanism a beginner needs is that the token is a record of a claim, not the asset: for a tokenised money market fund the token represents a fund share, the Treasuries stay with the fund, transfers run only between approved wallets, and the BIS classes these as securities with money-market-fund liquidity risks ([BIS Bulletin 115](https://www.bis.org/publ/bisbull115.htm)). IOSCO's assessment, "growing but remains nascent", with legal uncertainty among the risks, is quotable from the reproduced release ([IOSCO via Mondo Visione](https://mondovisione.com/media-and-resources/news/iosco-publishes-final-report-on-financial-asset-tokenization-20251111/)). The dated layer: $38.73bn of transferable tokenised assets excluding stablecoins and $14.82bn of tokenised Treasuries on 4 October 2026, the latter down 6.6% in 30 days, so "always growing" is not safe ([rwa.xyz](https://app.rwa.xyz/); [rwa.xyz](https://app.rwa.xyz/treasuries)). The UK thread is strong and dated: the Digital Securities Sandbox excludes "cryptocurrencies like Bitcoin" and runs until 8 January 2029 ([Bank of England](https://www.bankofengland.co.uk/financial-stability/digital-securities-sandbox)), with 16 entrants and two live permissions ([Bank of England](https://www.bankofengland.co.uk/financial-stability/digital-securities-sandbox/digital-securities-sandbox-dashboard)); the first digital gilt is due by Q1 2027 on HSBC's platform ([HM Treasury](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/update-on-the-digital-gilt-instrument-digit-pilot-issuance/update-on-the-digital-gilt-instrument-digit-pilot-issuance)); the FCA's fund tokenisation rules took effect on 30 April 2026 ([FCA PS26/7](https://www.fca.org.uk/publication/policy/ps26-7.pdf)); and tokenised sterling deposits, which are bank deposits not stablecoins, finished a retail pilot in September 2026 ([UK Finance](https://www.ukfinance.org.uk/tokenised-sterling-deposits)). The honest framing for a UK beginner is that most of this is institutional plumbing they cannot yet buy. Hold BUIDL's terms, BENJI's structure, the "whitelisted investors" sentence attributed to IOSCO, JPMorgan's deposit token, tokenised stock eligibility and the exact rwa.xyz definitions.

### Scams and UK tax sit outside the ten

The research on both is among the strongest in the set: seven scam mechanics, each sourced step by step to the FCA, FINRA, the FTC, Chainalysis, Ledger and Scam Sniffer, and an HMRC-sourced account of disposals, pooling, income and reporting. Neither has a lesson. Four of the ten cannot be taught honestly without them. Lesson 2 must say that the recovery phrase is what fake-support scammers ask for. Lesson 3 must show address poisoning. Lesson 6 must separate registered, authorised and compensated. Lessons 7, 8 and 9 each describe an action HMRC treats as taxable: a swap is a disposal ([gov.uk](https://www.gov.uk/guidance/check-if-you-need-to-pay-tax-when-you-sell-cryptoassets)), staking rewards are income at receipt ([HMRC](https://www.gov.uk/hmrc-internal-manuals/cryptoassets-manual/crypto21200)), and a DeFi deposit can be a disposal ([HMRC](https://www.gov.uk/hmrc-internal-manuals/cryptoassets-manual/crypto61620)). The minimum is a sourced one-line signpost inside each of those lessons; the fuller treatment, which the research can support as two standalone lessons, needs an accountant's review of the tax wording and a lawyer's review of the regulatory wording, as the researchers flagged. Any external page the lessons link to should be checked for UK availability, since at least two educational sites were geo-restricted when fetched.

## Conclusion

The site's rule, true and sourced or not used, turns out to sort crypto content by half-life rather than by topic. Mechanisms have a long half-life and primary sources: how a block links, how a key becomes an address, how a pool prices a swap, how a health factor triggers a liquidation. Magnitudes have a half-life of weeks: supply totals moved $20bn between trackers on one day, Aave's value more than halved in a year, DEX share set a record while DEX volume fell, and the staking rate drifts down as more ETH is staked. The build should reflect that split: lesson scripts carry the mechanism and example numbers, while every real figure lives in a dated, replaceable layer with its tracker named. The same logic applies to the corrections. The popular myths are wrong, but the popular corrections ("immutable", "not your keys, not your coins", "lost keys are gone forever") are wrong too, and the sourced versions are conditional: infeasible rather than impossible; whoever controls the keys controls the funds; recovery exists only where it was set up in advance.

The calendar of what will change is already written in the sources, and it doubles as a review schedule. The FCA's next consumer survey is due around December 2026 if its pattern holds; the Bank of England's stablecoin Code is to be finalised by the end of 2026; the US stablecoin law takes effect no later than 18 January 2027; the first digital gilt is due by Q1 2027; UK platforms report 2026 transactions to HMRC by 31 May 2027; the draft DeFi tax relief would start on 6 April 2027; the UK authorisation regime goes live on 25 October 2027; and Bitcoin's next halving arrives at block 1,050,000, around April 2028. Each of those dates will invalidate a sentence in at least one lesson, and three of them (the Code, the US rules and the regime start) will move claims from "hold" or "dated" into "safe". Scheduling the first full review for early 2027, with a lawyer reading the regulatory wording and an accountant the tax wording, is what the research itself recommends.
